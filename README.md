# Theological Foundations Program

**Status:** active methodological development / preservation-first

The Theological Foundations Program (TFP) is a working research space for systematic comparison of theological claims, frameworks, and interpretations across scriptural, historical, philosophical, doctrinal, archaeological, and other relevant evidence.

The project is inspired by the rigor sought in comparative framework research, but it is **not** assumed that theology should simply copy methods designed for physics.

## Governing aim

> Determine what theological claims, interpretive frameworks, and doctrinal structures are best supported by the relevant evidence and arguments, while making assumptions, standards, disagreements, and uncertainty explicit.

The project begins without presuming that all traditions converge, that one existing system is correct, or that theological questions are reducible to scientific ones.

## Initial evidence domains

Potential evidence domains include:

- biblical and other primary texts;
- textual criticism and manuscript history;
- historical context;
- early Jewish and Christian sources;
- church history;
- doctrinal development;
- philosophy of religion;
- metaphysics;
- ethics;
- archaeology;
- comparative interpretation;
- linguistics and translation;
- claims about religious experience;
- natural theology;
- arguments concerning miracles and revelation.

Not every question will admit every evidence type.

## Core epistemic boundary

The project must distinguish:

- **textual claim** — what a source says;
- **historical claim** — what likely occurred;
- **interpretive claim** — what a text or event means;
- **philosophical claim** — what follows from argument;
- **doctrinal claim** — what a theological system asserts;
- **empirical claim** — what can be investigated through observational methods;
- **faith commitment** — what may exceed publicly adjudicable evidence.

These categories may interact, but they must not be silently collapsed.

## Relationship to belief

This repository may examine Christianity seriously and sympathetically without treating desired conclusions as established evidence.

It should be possible for an investigation to:

- strengthen a claim;
- narrow it;
- distinguish tradition from source;
- identify unresolved disagreement;
- reject an argument;
- or conclude that available evidence does not decide the question.

## Relationship to other projects

TFP exists in the same global reality as the rest of the portfolio but is not created to validate NFC, PGH, FCP, ISR, or any other project.

Any structural bridge must be independently established.
