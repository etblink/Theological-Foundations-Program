# Theological Foundations Program

**Status:** active truth-adjudication program; qualified method in maintenance mode; first canonical bounded adjudication complete

The Theological Foundations Program (TFP) is a research program for systematic comparison and adjudication of theological claims, interpretive frameworks, historical claims, philosophical arguments, doctrinal systems, and related evidence.

## Governing aim

> Determine which theological claims, interpretive frameworks, and doctrinal structures, **if any**, are true—or closest to the truth available to us—while making evidence, assumptions, standards, disagreements, uncertainty, and unresolved underdetermination explicit.

TFP does not presume Christianity, naturalism, supernaturalism, skepticism, or any other framework true in advance.

Possible legitimate outcomes include support for one framework, partial truth distributed across frameworks, revision or synthesis, rejection of examined candidates, or genuine underdetermination.

## Governing maxim

> **The protocol exists to help TFP discover what is true. TFP does not exist to perfect the protocol.**

Protocol repair is now primarily maintenance work driven by defects exposed in substantive research, not a standing invitation to endless methodology development.

## Core epistemic boundaries

TFP keeps distinct:

- textual claims — what a source says;
- historical claims — what likely occurred;
- interpretive claims — what a text or event means;
- philosophical claims — what follows from argument;
- doctrinal claims — what a theological system asserts;
- empirical claims — what can be investigated observationally;
- existential/practical consequences;
- faith commitments.

These categories may interact but must not silently substitute for one another.

In particular:

- a historical or natural explanation of how a belief arose does not by itself show the belief false;
- antiquity, continuity, sincerity, religious importance, or cultural persistence do not show a belief true;
- local study results do not silently become global theological conclusions.

## Repository hygiene

The repository intentionally preserves historical protocol versions, audits, dispositions, EMT artifacts, and research branches because they carry provenance and negative knowledge.

Housekeeping should therefore prefer:

- updating orientation/index material rather than rewriting frozen artifacts;
- additive correction rather than historical mutation;
- preserving branch-qualified references used by canonical STATE;
- removing or consolidating only genuinely incidental material that carries no provenance or active reference;
- avoiding documentation growth that does not improve research execution or cold-start clarity.

Older artifacts may look stale because they are historical snapshots. Their presence does not make them current authority.
