## Candidate-native / primary formulation

Relevant contingent reality ultimately obtains without a deeper truth-relevant explanation being required or available. The family does not say merely that investigators have not yet found an explanation. It says at least one ultimate contingent fact, totality, or stopping structure is genuinely brute.

The admitted nomological subvariant permits a law-governed stopping structure to be contingent: laws or nomic structure may organize and explain derivative facts while the ultimate nomological setup itself has no deeper explanation.

## Literature status

This is a direct, recognizable position in the literature on cosmological explanation and the Principle of Sufficient Reason, though authors differ over whether the brute item is the universe, a total contingent fact, laws, or another stopping fact.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **Bertrand Russell**, 1948 BBC debate with F. C. Copleston, reprinted in *Why I Am Not a Christian and Other Essays* (1964). Russell rejects the demand for an explanation of the universe as a whole and treats the universe as simply there.
2. **Graham Oppy**, “O’Connor’s Cosmological Argument,” in *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion*, vol. 3 (2011), pp. 166–186. Oppy treats brute contingency as one of the serious naturalistic options for ultimate explanation.
3. **Yitzhak Y. Melamed and Martin Lin**, “Principle of Sufficient Reason,” *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (rev. 2023), for the live dispute over unrestricted PSR and brute facts.

These sources anchor the family but do not determine every TFP subvariant.

## Core truth propositions

1. Relevant contingent reality obtains.
2. At least one ultimate contingent fact, totality, or law-governed stopping structure lacks a deeper truth-relevant explanation.
3. The lack of deeper explanation is ontic, not merely epistemic.
4. No principle of explanation valid within the domain is automatically entitled to demand an explanation of the ultimate stopping fact.

## Authority commitments

None. This is a philosophical/metaphysical position. It does not depend on scripture, ecclesial authority, revelation, or a privileged scientific theory.

## Internal success conditions

The family succeeds on its own terms if:

- brute termination is coherent rather than a disguised contradiction;
- the stopping point does not covertly rely on a necessary ground;
- the position can distinguish a genuine brute fact from temporary ignorance;
- local or derivative explanations can remain legitimate even if ultimate explanation terminates;
- the nomological subvariant does not silently convert contingent laws into metaphysically necessary principles.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

Candidate-native expectation is primarily negative and structural rather than a prediction of a particular observation. The view is compatible with rich local explanation while denying that every explanatory chain must terminate in a deeper reason. It would be strengthened by a defensible limit on PSR-style principles or by showing that ultimate explanatory stopping is not epistemically or metaphysically defective merely because it is contingent.

No empirical result is frozen here as confirmatory evidence; G1 remains unauthorized.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **PSR / explanatory-arbitrariness objection:** if the stopping fact is contingent, why does it obtain rather than not? Calling it brute may restate rather than answer the question.
2. **Selective stopping objection:** a proponent must explain why explanatory demands are legitimate for derivative contingent facts but not the ultimate contingent fact.
3. **Nomological relocation objection:** placing brute status in laws or a law-governed structure may relocate rather than remove the unexplained contingency.
4. **Comparative-explanatory objection:** if a coherent deeper explanation is available, brute termination may lose on explanatory adequacy even if it remains logically possible.
5. **Epistemic masking objection:** apparent brutality may merely reflect current ignorance; the candidate therefore needs a principled ontic/epistemic distinction.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- “Brute” does **not** mean “we do not know yet.”
- The family does **not** claim that nothing causes ordinary events.
- It does **not** entail naturalism, atheism, materialism, or scientism.
- It does **not** require the universe to be temporally beginningless.
- It does **not** claim laws are unexplained in every subvariant.
- It does **not** establish that asking “why?” is meaningless in all contexts.
