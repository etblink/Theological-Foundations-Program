# TFP Program — Next Substantive Study Selection 0.1.0

**Status:** PROGRAM-LEVEL STUDY-SELECTION PROPOSAL — NO G0 AUTHORIZATION  
**Date:** 2026-10-06  
**Authority:** Program Lead proposal only; human owner retains G0 authorization authority.

## 1. Re-anchor

TFP exists to discover which theological claims, interpretive frameworks, and doctrinal structures, if any, are true or closest to truth.

The protocol exists to help TFP discover what is true. TFP does not exist to perfect the protocol.

TFP-STRESS-2 is canonically CLOSED and must not be reopened absent a preserved reopen trigger.

The next study should therefore maximize new contact with the larger theological truth problem rather than repeat the same historical-resurrection evidentiary ecology.

## 2. Selection criteria

A strong next study should:

1. be genuinely substantive rather than methodological;
2. be bounded enough for a complete G0-G6 cycle;
3. be methodologically orthogonal to TFP-STRESS-2 where possible;
4. bear on multiple broad frameworks rather than only one denomination or doctrine;
5. avoid assuming Christianity, naturalism, supernaturalism, or skepticism;
6. have serious live rival positions that can be steelmanned under equal standards;
7. add information useful to later cross-study theological synthesis;
8. avoid unnecessary dependence on NFC, ISR, EMT, or other external projects;
9. be capable of yielding support, defeat, or underdetermination without forcing a binary answer.

No outcome-relevant evidence search has been performed for this selection memo.

## 3. Candidate A — Divine hiddenness / perfect love

### Candidate question

Does the existence and distribution of apparently nonresistant nonbelief materially count against the existence of a perfectly loving personal God, once serious theistic and non-theistic explanations are compared under equal standards?

### Strengths

- strongly orthogonal to the historical/source-critical structure of TFP-STRESS-2;
- tests philosophical, experiential, anthropological, and theological reasoning;
- bears on broad personal-theism frameworks, not only Christianity;
- naturally admits strong rival explanations;
- can be bounded without requiring a global adjudication of God's existence;
- highly relevant to later synthesis about personal theism, revelation, and religious diversity.

### Risks

- "nonresistant" and "perfectly loving" require careful operationalization;
- empirical distribution evidence can become too broad if not tightly scoped;
- may generate framework-dependence rather than a clean comparative result.

### Program value

HIGH.

## 4. Candidate B — Evidential problem of evil / suffering

### Candidate question

Does the observed distribution and character of apparently gratuitous suffering materially count against the existence of an omnipotent, omniscient, perfectly good personal God, after strong theistic and non-theistic accounts are compared?

### Strengths

- central to broad theism;
- substantial philosophical and empirical content;
- highly important to later synthesis;
- strong rival literatures exist.

### Risks

- potentially enormous scope;
- "gratuitous" can become definitionally loaded;
- animal suffering, natural evil, moral evil, skeptical theism, soul-making, free will, afterlife compensation, and unknown-goods arguments can make one study sprawling.

### Program value

HIGH, but harder to bound cleanly than Candidate A.

## 5. Candidate C — Contingency / necessary foundation

### Candidate question

Does the existence of contingent reality warrant a necessary explanatory foundation, and if so, how much of classical or personal theism follows from that result?

### Strengths

- highly orthogonal to TFP-STRESS-2;
- directly tests metaphysical reasoning;
- useful for broad theism/non-theism comparison;
- potentially precise.

### Risks

- heavy dependence on contested modal/metaphysical premises;
- easy to slide from "necessary foundation" to "God" without warrant;
- may have limited discriminatory power among impersonal and personal ultimate-foundation candidates.

### Program value

MEDIUM-HIGH.

## 6. Candidate D — Revelation / scriptural authority

### Candidate question

What, if anything, warrants treating a body of claimed revelation as epistemically authoritative rather than merely historically important or religiously meaningful?

### Strengths

- central to Christianity and other revealed traditions;
- would eventually be necessary for doctrinal adjudication;
- exercises textual, historical, epistemological, and theological lanes.

### Risks

- too broad unless one revelation corpus is selected;
- authority criteria may depend on prior theism and miracle adjudications;
- risks prematurely collapsing into denominational comparison.

### Program value

HIGH eventually, but not ideal as the immediate second canonical study.

## 7. Candidate E — Jesus' self-understanding / divine-status claims

### Candidate question

What is the strongest historically warranted account of Jesus' own self-understanding with respect to messianic, eschatological, and divine-status claims?

### Strengths

- high relevance to Christianity;
- evidence base is identifiable;
- naturally builds toward later Christological adjudication.

### Risks

- too close methodologically to TFP-STRESS-2;
- repeats source-critical/historical machinery before testing TFP in another domain;
- can create an overly Christianity-centered early program trajectory.

### Program value

HIGH, but strategically premature.

## 8. Program-Lead recommendation

**Recommend Candidate A: Divine hiddenness / perfect love as the next bounded substantive study.**

Reason:

TFP-STRESS-2 established that the program can execute a difficult historical/testimonial/miracle-adjacent adjudication without collapsing into apologetics or reductive skepticism. The best next test is therefore not another early-Christian historical question.

Divine hiddenness gives TFP a substantially different evidentiary and argumentative ecology while remaining central to the broad question of whether a personal, perfectly loving God exists. It can produce information relevant to Christianity, Judaism, Islam, philosophical theism, deism, impersonal ultimate-reality views, agnosticism, and naturalism without presuming any of them.

A later cross-study synthesis would be much more informative if it combined:
- a bounded resurrection-event result;
- a bounded personal-theism/hiddenness result;
- eventually an evil/suffering or positive-natural-theology result;
- and only then more specifically Christian revelation/Christology studies.
