## Candidate-native / primary formulation

Every relevant contingent explanandum is explained by further contingent reality in a non-terminating one-way dependence chain. There is no terminal brute contingent fact and no necessary explanatory ground. Explanatory adequacy is distributed through the regress rather than supplied by a terminal member.

The family concerns explanatory dependence, not merely an infinite temporal past.

## Literature status

This is a direct, recognizable option in cosmological-argument literature, though proponents may differ over whether the regress is causal, grounding-based, temporal, or more general.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **David Hume**, *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*, Part IX (1779). Hume presses the thought that an eternal series whose members are explained by preceding members need not receive a further explanation as one whole.
2. **Graham Oppy**, “O’Connor’s Cosmological Argument,” *Oxford Studies in Philosophy of Religion* 3 (2011), 166–186. Oppy treats infinite regress as one of the serious naturalistic alternatives to a transcendent necessary being.
3. *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, “Cosmological Argument,” for contemporary treatment of regress-based objections and the distinction between explaining members and demanding a terminus.

Hume’s presentation also overlaps with global-deflation themes; this packet isolates the one-way non-terminating dependence commitment.

## Core truth propositions

1. Relevant contingent reality obtains.
2. Each relevant contingent explanandum has or can have a further contingent explanans.
3. The dependence chain has no terminal member.
4. The regress is not itself treated as one brute totality.
5. Explanatory adequacy does not require a necessary terminus.

## Authority commitments

None. The family is philosophical/metaphysical and does not rely on religious authority.

## Internal success conditions

- Infinite explanatory descent must be coherent.
- Each link must supply genuine explanatory work rather than mere correlation or repetition.
- The regress must not covertly rely on an unmentioned terminating structure.
- The total explanatory arrangement must not collapse into C-BRUTE by treating the entire regress as an unexplained contingent fact.
- The view must explain why well-foundedness is not required for explanation.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

The position expects no discoverable necessary terminus to be required merely from the fact that each contingent item is dependent. Its strongest support is structural: demonstration that non-terminating dependence can remain explanatory at every stage and that the demand for a final ground is an additional premise, not a logical consequence of contingency.

No G1 evidence claim is frozen here.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Totality objection:** even if every member is explained by another member, why does this entire regress obtain rather than another or none?
2. **Well-foundedness objection:** explanation may require asymmetric dependence ultimately anchored in something non-dependent.
3. **Deferred-explanation objection:** an endless series may perpetually postpone rather than complete explanation.
4. **Modal-contingency objection:** if every member is contingent, the entire arrangement may remain contingently obtaining and hence apparently still call for explanation.
5. **Transmission objection:** derivative explanatory power may not be generated merely by passing dependence indefinitely backward.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- The family is **not** simply “an infinite past.”
- It is not committed to every causal chain being infinite.
- It does not say the regress is one unexplained brute totality.
- It does not deny that individual contingent facts can be explained.
- It does not entail naturalism or atheism.
- It does not claim infinity itself explains anything; the explanatory claim concerns the links.
