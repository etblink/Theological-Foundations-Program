# Theological Foundations Program — Program Charter 0.1.0

**Status:** governing program anchor  
**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Authority:** restatement of the founding purpose already preserved in README.md and RESEARCH_SEED_0_1_0.md

## 1. Program purpose

The Theological Foundations Program exists to build a disciplined, theology-native research method for comparing theological claims, interpretive frameworks, historical claims, philosophical arguments, doctrinal systems, and related evidence.

Its governing aim is:

> Determine what theological claims, interpretive frameworks, and doctrinal structures are best supported by the relevant evidence and arguments, while making assumptions, standards, disagreements, and uncertainty explicit.

The program is analogous in **seriousness** to rigorous comparative research elsewhere, but not necessarily in method.

Theology is not physics with different nouns.

## 2. Governing research posture

The motivating question is not:

> How can an already preferred theology be defended?

It is:

> What theological claims and frameworks survive careful comparison against the kinds of evidence and argument actually relevant to them?

Christianity may be the initial major object of study because it is historically rich, internally diverse, and of particular interest.

That does not authorize assuming:

- Christianity is true;
- Christianity is false;
- one denomination is normative;
- later consensus perfectly preserves earliest belief;
- minority positions are correct merely because they are minority positions.

## 3. Claim-type separation

TFP must keep distinct:

- textual claims;
- historical claims;
- interpretive claims;
- philosophical claims;
- doctrinal claims;
- empirical claims;
- existential/practical consequences;
- faith commitments.

These may interact.

They must not silently substitute for one another.

Examples:

- historical evidence that a belief existed is not evidence that the belief is true;
- doctrinal coherence is not external truth;
- textual meaning is not identical to later doctrinal use;
- philosophical possibility is not actuality;
- a natural historical origin does not by itself falsify a theological claim;
- a theological claim being true does not make every historical explanation associated with it correct.

## 4. Core principles

### Equal-standard principle
Comparable rival claims should face comparable evidential and argumentative burdens.

### Claim-type principle
Standards must match the kind of claim under investigation.

### Source-proximity principle
Later interpretation must not silently replace earlier evidence when the question concerns earlier sources.

### Negative-evidence principle
Failed arguments, rejected hypotheses, negative findings, and unresolved disagreements are preserved.

### Steelman principle
Positions should be tested in forms informed proponents could recognize.

### Scope principle
A finding about one text, doctrine, argument, tradition, or period does not automatically generalize to a whole religion.

### Belief/research distinction
Personal belief may motivate inquiry but must not determine research disposition.

### Anti-reduction principle
Theology may be illuminated by archaeology, history, sociology, psychology, literary criticism, philosophy, or science without being silently reduced to any one of them.

## 5. Foundational methodological problem

Before large doctrinal conclusions, TFP must develop and stress-test a method capable of answering:

> How can a theology-native research program compare competing claims without becoming disguised apologetics and without importing inappropriate standards from unrelated disciplines?

The current EMT investigation serves as one **methodological stress test** of that problem.

It is not the program's final subject matter.

## 6. EMT boundary

The Environmental–Monumental–Theological Development Hypothesis (EMT) is:

- a bounded investigation;
- a source of methodological lessons;
- a test of claim decomposition;
- a test of interdisciplinary evidence handling;
- a test of historical-genealogy reasoning.

EMT is **not**:

- the governing theory of TFP;
- the definition of theology;
- authorization to reduce religion to environmental history;
- authorization to make ancient Egypt the permanent center of the project.

When EMT reaches a useful closure point, TFP must return to program-level method design and later comparative theological questions.

## 7. Future program scope remains broad

Potential future work may include:

- scriptural authority;
- canon;
- resurrection claims;
- Trinitarian models;
- Christology;
- atonement;
- justification and sanctification;
- sacramental theology;
- ecclesiology;
- eschatology;
- divine hiddenness;
- problem of evil;
- free will and providence;
- natural theology;
- miracles and revelation;
- moral realism;
- denominational disagreement;
- Jewish–Christian continuity and discontinuity;
- non-Christian comparison where the question requires it.

This list is illustrative, not a frozen roadmap.

## 8. Relationship to ISR

Invariant Structure Research remains parked during the current TFP independence interval.

TFP should develop its own vocabulary, methods, evidence distinctions, and failure modes.

No structural similarity to physics, software, markets, or other projects establishes theological truth.

> Seek the strongest account of what is true, while preserving enough distinction among source, history, interpretation, argument, doctrine, evidence, and faith that one cannot quietly masquerade as another.
