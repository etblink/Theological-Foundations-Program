# Environmental–Monumental–Theological Development Hypothesis — Preregistration 0.1.0

**Project:** Theological Foundations Program  
**Status:** preregistered hypothesis; investigation authorized  
**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Purpose:** freeze the proposed developmental chain before substantive evidence review

## Governing rule

This file records the hypothesis before research adjudication.

Later evidence may strengthen, narrow, redirect, or reject any link. The hypothesis must not be silently rewritten to match the evidence.

## Proposed chain

```text
environmental observation
→ storm/rain/fertility association
→ religious interpretation
→ special significance of lightning-associated places or objects
→ deliberate incorporation of such objects/places into ritual practice
→ increasingly elaborate stone/monumental construction
→ specialist architectural, surveying, and geometric knowledge
→ intercultural transmission of technical and symbolic traditions
→ Greek/Roman/Hellenistic development
→ interaction with Jewish and Egyptian traditions
→ contribution to later theological narratives
```

## Frozen claim set

### H1 — Environmental association
Ancient people noticed recurring relationships among storms, rain, fertility, vegetation, and harvests.

### H2 — Religious encoding
Those observed relationships contributed to storm, thunder, lightning, rain, and fertility deities or sacred symbolism.

### H3 — Preferential-strike recognition
Ancient people noticed that lightning appeared to favor particular elevated places, trees, rocks, mountains, or exposed locations.

### H4 — Lightning-associated material culture
Objects or places associated with lightning acquired special ritual, sacred, collectible, transportable, or preservational significance.

### H5 — Deliberate ritual placement
Some lightning-associated stones or objects were deliberately moved, positioned, or incorporated into elevated or sacred settings.

### H6 — Architectural elaboration
Practices involving significant stones, high places, weather, storm symbolism, or ritual placement contributed to increasingly elaborate stone constructions.

### H7 — Monumental-development bridge
Some monumental forms, potentially including artificial mounds, pyramidal monuments, obelisks, stone circles, or comparable raised structures, partly developed from that broad ritual/technical trajectory.

### H8 — Technical specialization
Monument building fostered increasingly sophisticated architecture, surveying, measurement, and geometry.

### H9 — Knowledge transmission
Relevant Egyptian and/or other ancient Near Eastern technical knowledge influenced Greek intellectual and architectural traditions, with later transmission into Roman traditions.

### H10 — Hellenistic synthesis
Greek/Hellenistic, Jewish, and Egyptian intellectual-religious traditions interacted substantially.

### H11 — Later theological contribution
Some elements produced through those interactions contributed to later theological narratives or doctrinal development.

## Dependency rule

Each hypothesis is adjudicated independently.

A failure of H5, for example, does not automatically falsify H1–H4 or H8–H11. It does, however, weaken or break the proposed continuous genealogy linking them.

## Evidence classes

For each hypothesis, distinguish:

- physical plausibility;
- archaeological evidence;
- material-culture evidence;
- textual attestation;
- iconographic evidence;
- historical inference;
- later folklore or reception history;
- modern scholarly interpretation.

Later folklore must not be projected backward into antiquity without evidence.

## Anti-confirmation rule

Research must actively seek:

- alternative explanations;
- chronology conflicts;
- absence of expected evidence;
- later folklore mistakenly treated as ancient evidence;
- independent invention;
- multifunctional monument explanations;
- reverse or multidirectional cultural transmission.
