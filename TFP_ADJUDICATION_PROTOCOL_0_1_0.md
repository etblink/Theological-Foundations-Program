**Project:** Theological Foundations Program  
**Status:** candidate protocol — requires independent adversarial audit before canonical qualification  
**Date:** 2026-10-05  
**Governance:** `GOVERNANCE.md`

## 1. Purpose

This protocol defines how TFP should move from a theological truth question to a bounded, auditable adjudication.

Its goal is not merely to identify which position is easiest to defend.

Its goal is to determine, as far as the available evidence and reasoning permit:

> Which candidate claim or framework, if any, is true—or closest to the truth we are warranted in identifying?

The protocol is intentionally plural in method because theology contains heterogeneous claim types.

A manuscript claim, historical-event claim, metaphysical argument, doctrinal-development claim, and revelation claim cannot be responsibly forced through one evidential instrument.

## 2. Required outputs of a study

Every fully adjudicated study should preserve:

1. **Question charter**
2. **Candidate universe**
3. **Claim-type map**
4. **Source/evidence map**
5. **Preregistered discriminators**
6. **Independent evidence-lane reports**
7. **Candidate steelman records**
8. **Comparative synthesis**
9. **Adversarial audit**
10. **Bounded adjudication**
11. **Uncertainty statement**
12. **Negative knowledge**
13. **Stop/reopen conditions**
14. **Canonical acceptance record**, if accepted

A study may stop earlier if the evidence is insufficient or the question is malformed.

## 3. Phase A — Truth-question charter

Before major evidence review, freeze the question.

The charter must state:

### A1 — Exact truth question
Avoid vague topics.

Bad:
> Study the Trinity.

Better:
> Which account of the relation among Father, Son, and Spirit is best warranted by the earliest relevant textual evidence, subsequent historical development, and the philosophical commitments required by each serious candidate?

### A2 — Scope
State:
- time period;
- source corpus;
- traditions included;
- geographic scope;
- languages where relevant;
- downstream claims explicitly excluded.

### A3 — Claim types
List every major claim type involved.

### A4 — Candidate-selection rule
State how candidates enter the comparison.

### A5 — Stop conditions
Define what would count as:
- enough evidence to adjudicate;
- genuine underdetermination;
- insufficient signal;
- dependence on unavailable evidence.

### A6 — Reopen conditions
Specify what could materially change the result.

## 4. Phase B — Candidate universe

The candidate universe must be constructed before comparative closure.

Candidate classes may include:

- inherited doctrinal positions;
- historically attested minority positions;
- skeptical or naturalistic accounts;
- philosophical alternatives;
- hybrid/revised syntheses;
- `NONE_ADEQUATE`;
- `UNDERDETERMINED`.

### Candidate admission rule

A candidate should normally be admitted if it is:

1. seriously represented in relevant scholarship or tradition; or
2. independently generated by the evidence and sufficiently specified to test; and
3. materially distinct from already admitted candidates.

### Candidate exclusion rule

A candidate may be excluded if it is:

- merely verbal variation;
- dependent on assumptions already rejected within scope;
- too underspecified to generate discriminating expectations;
- historically irrelevant to a historical-development question unless explicitly included as a modern truth candidate.

Exclusions must be documented.

## 5. Phase C — Independent candidate construction

Where practical, each major candidate should be constructed in its strongest recognizable form before comparative attack.

Preferred evidence:

- candidate-native primary sources;
- respected scholarly proponents;
- official confessional texts where the candidate is institutional;
- serious philosophical formulations.

Avoid defining one candidate entirely through an opponent's critique.

### Independence safeguard

Where contamination risk is high:
- freeze candidate packets before cross-comparison;
- use separate researchers/agents;
- delay access to rival packets;
- preserve source boundaries.

## 6. Phase D — Claim-type matrix

Each major proposition should be classified.

| Claim type | Core question | Typical evidence | Typical failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Textual | What does the source say? | manuscripts, critical editions, grammar, genre, context | later doctrine projected backward |
| Historical | What likely occurred? | testimony, chronology, archaeology, multiple attestation, context | possibility treated as actuality |
| Archaeological/material | What does material context establish? | provenance, stratigraphy, dating, laboratory data | artifact meaning overread |
| Linguistic | What does wording/term/history support? | philology, semantic range, comparative corpus | dictionary gloss treated as fixed meaning |
| Interpretive | What does a text/event mean? | context, discourse, intertext, reception | circular reading |
| Philosophical | What follows from premises? | formal/informal argument, modal analysis, conceptual coherence | hidden premise or equivocation |
| Doctrinal | What does a system assert and entail? | creeds, councils, confessions, theologians | system coherence mistaken for truth |
| Empirical | What is observationally testable? | measured data, reproducibility | theology substituted for mechanism |
| Experiential | What does testimony/experience support? | testimony, phenomenology, psychology | sincerity mistaken for external truth |
| Faith commitment | What is accepted beyond public adjudication? | confessional commitment | treated as public evidence without argument |

A claim may occupy multiple rows.

The study must state which evidence class is carrying which inference.

## 7. Phase E — Source hierarchy rules

TFP does not use one universal source hierarchy.

The hierarchy is question-relative.

### Textual question
Prefer, where relevant:
1. earliest/most reliable manuscript evidence;
2. critical text;
3. linguistic/contextual analysis;
4. early reception as secondary evidence;
5. later doctrinal use.

### Historical question
Prefer:
1. contemporaneous or near-contemporaneous evidence;
2. independent source streams;
3. archaeological/material controls;
4. contextual plausibility;
5. later testimony with explicit discount for temporal distance/dependence.

### Doctrinal-development question
Prefer:
1. source at the period being studied;
2. identifiable transmission/carrier evidence;
3. intermediate formulations;
4. later systematizations;
5. retrospective harmonizations last.

### Philosophical question
Authority by antiquity or institution does not determine validity.

Assess:
- premises;
- validity;
- soundness;
- explanatory reach;
- internal consistency;
- rival arguments.

### Revelation/miracle question
Do not:
- assume naturalism;
- suspend ordinary historical standards.

Separate:
1. historical evidence;
2. candidate natural explanations;
3. philosophical admissibility of non-natural causation;
4. theological identification of cause.

## 8. Phase F — Evidence-strength representation

Avoid one global numerical score.

Each important proposition should instead record:

- **quality** of evidence;
- **independence**;
- **proximity**;
- **directness**;
- **provenance**;
- **replicability** where applicable;
- **discriminatory power**;
- **major alternatives**.

Suggested qualitative dispositions:

- `SUPPORTED`
- `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED`
- `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_UNATTESTED`
- `NOT_ESTABLISHED`
- `EVIDENCE_AGAINST`
- `CONTRADICTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
- `UNDERDETERMINED`
- `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL`

These labels do not replace reasoning.

Every consequential disposition requires a rationale.

## 9. Phase G — Preregistered discriminators

Before comparative synthesis, identify evidence that would actually distinguish candidates.

A discriminator should answer:

> If Candidate A and Candidate B were both compatible with fact X, what observation or argument would make us prefer one over the other?

Weak discriminator:
> Candidate A can explain X.

Strong discriminator:
> Candidate A predicts X while Candidate B predicts not-X, or Candidate B requires an additional unsupported assumption to accommodate X.

Where no discriminators exist, the study may be intrinsically underdetermined.

## 10. Phase H — Independent evidence lanes

Evidence should be analyzed in separate lanes before synthesis where cross-contamination is a risk.

Example for a resurrection study:

- Lane T — textual transmission/composition;
- Lane H — historical core;
- Lane A — alternative historical explanations;
- Lane P — philosophy of testimony/miracle;
- Lane D — theological inference.

Rules:

1. Lane conclusions must stay within lane competence.
2. A lane may cite another lane's accepted evidence but not silently inherit its conclusion.
3. Conflicts are recorded, not harmonized automatically.
4. Evidence collection should precede cross-lane narrative construction.

## 11. Phase I — Continuity Ladder

For historical/doctrinal development claims, use:

- **C0** recurrence only
- **C1** material/form continuity
- **C2** carrier continuity
- **C3** ritual/practice continuity
- **C4** semantic continuity
- **C5** named textual continuity
- **C6** genealogical continuity
- **C7** doctrinal continuity

A later doctrine may reach C7 locally without proving the truth of the doctrine.

The ladder adjudicates historical continuity, not metaphysical truth.

## 12. Phase J — Independent-reinvention null

Whenever a concept could plausibly arise multiple times from common conditions, test independent invention.

Examples:
- flood → fertility;
- divine creator concepts;
- afterlife journey imagery;
- moral reciprocity;
- sacrificial symbolism;
- triadic language.

Transmission requires evidence beyond resemblance.

## 13. Phase K — Equal-standard challenge

For every major criticism of Candidate A:

1. identify the general rule behind the criticism;
2. apply that rule to Candidate B and other candidates where relevant;
3. record asymmetric consequences.

Examples:

If late development counts against one doctrine, ask whether rivals also depend on later development.

If unexplained testimony counts against a skeptical account, ask whether the theological account incurs unexplained assumptions elsewhere.

If institutional consensus is invoked for one position, state why consensus should be evidentially relevant in that question.

## 14. Phase L — Internal adequacy vs external warrant

Evaluate separately:

### Internal adequacy
Given the candidate's premises:
- is it coherent?
- does it explain its own sources?
- does it avoid contradiction?
- does it solve its internal problems?

### External warrant
Are the candidate's premises themselves supported?

A framework may be internally elegant but externally weak.

A historically well-supported claim may sit inside a broader system that remains underdetermined.

## 15. Phase M — Origins / development / meaning / truth

Every major theological genealogy must answer separately:

### Origins
How did the claim arise?

### Development
How did it change or become institutionalized?

### Meaning
What did participants mean in relevant contexts?

### Truth
Is the proposition actually true?

No result in the first three categories automatically decides the fourth.

## 16. Phase N — Synthesis

Only after evidence lanes are frozen should comparative synthesis begin.

The synthesis must include:

1. strongest evidence for each candidate;
2. strongest evidence against each;
3. unresolved anomalies;
4. assumptions needed by each;
5. discriminators won/lost;
6. candidate-universe completeness check;
7. possibility of hybrid/revised synthesis;
8. explicit `NONE_ADEQUATE` and `UNDERDETERMINED` checks.

The synthesis may propose a provisional adjudication.

It cannot canonize it.

## 17. Phase O — Adversarial audit

Before canonical acceptance, attempt to break the provisional conclusion.

Minimum audit questions:

1. Was the strongest rival steelmanned?
2. Did the favored candidate receive lower burdens?
3. Did source selection favor the conclusion?
4. Did later theology get projected backward?
5. Did a local result become a global inference?
6. Were alternative explanations actually tested?
7. Did candidate-universe construction omit a serious rival?
8. Did one framework define the test language?
9. Are key sources dependent rather than independent?
10. Would an unfamiliar competent researcher recover the same reasoning from the artifacts?

Possible audit outcomes:

- `PASS`
- `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`
- `REPAIR_REQUIRED`
- `INVALID_COMPARISON`
- `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL`

Audit PASS is necessary where the protocol requires it, but is not itself canonical acceptance.

## 18. Phase P — Canonical adjudication

A canonical adjudication record should contain:

- exact question;
- scope;
- accepted candidate set;
- claim-type map;
- key evidence;
- lane results;
- provisional synthesis;
- audit result;
- final bounded disposition;
- uncertainty;
- known weaknesses;
- negative knowledge;
- reopen conditions;
- date/version/provenance.

Possible bounded outcomes include:

- `CANDIDATE_A_BEST_SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
- `CANDIDATE_A_SUBSTANTIALLY_SUPPORTED`
- `HYBRID_OR_REVISED_MODEL_REQUIRED`
- `NONE_ADEQUATE`
- `UNDERDETERMINED`
- `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL`

A program-level statement such as:

> Christianity is true.

requires substantially more than one bounded adjudication.

## 19. Phase Q — Authorization transition

Canonical truth conclusions and future research authorization are distinct.

After a study is accepted, `STATE.yaml` may be updated to reflect:

- accepted bounded conclusion;
- negative knowledge;
- study status;
- next action;
- hold/reopen conditions.

The result does not automatically authorize:
- a wider doctrinal conclusion;
- a new research program;
- changes to the charter;
- or claims beyond scope.

## 20. Phase R — Stop and reopen

A study should stop when:

- the authorized question has a bounded answer;
- available evidence cannot discriminate candidates;
- the next material step depends on unavailable evidence;
- further work has low expected information value;
- or a higher-priority program gate dominates.

Every closure should state `reopen_if`.

## 21. Blinding menu

Use blindness only where it reduces a real bias.

Possible techniques:

- anonymize tradition labels;
- anonymize author identity;
- freeze semantic interpretation before revealing doctrinal affiliation;
- independent candidate construction;
- independent evidence lanes;
- held-out sources;
- second auditor receives no provisional verdict.

Do not blind away information genuinely necessary to interpretation.

## 22. Cold-start reproducibility

Before treating the protocol as mature, an unfamiliar competent researcher should be able to use the frozen artifacts and reach a recognizable reconstruction of:

- the question;
- candidates;
- evidence;
- reasoning;
- uncertainty;
- decision boundary.

If success depends on undocumented project lore, the protocol has failed succession.

## 23. Anti-heuristic stress tests

The protocol should eventually be tested on cases where shallow rules fail.

Examples of prohibited universal heuristics:

- always prefer the earliest source;
- always prefer consensus;
- always distrust later doctrine;
- always choose the simplest explanation;
- always choose the naturalistic explanation;
- always prefer miracle explanations when testimony is sincere;
- always treat doctrinal development as corruption;
- always treat doctrinal development as legitimate maturation.

A mature method must integrate evidence rather than substitute one slogan.
