# TFP-STUDY-3-CONTINGENCY — B3 Steelman Packet: C-NECESSITARIAN 0.1.0

## Identity

- Candidate: `C-NECESSITARIAN`
- Frozen label: **Necessary total reality / necessitarian totality**
- Status: **FROZEN_B3_PACKET__PENDING_EQUAL_STRENGTH_REVIEW**

## Candidate-native / primary formulation

The relevant total reality could not have failed to obtain. What appears contingent within our descriptions is internal, perspectival, derivative, epistemic, or otherwise compatible with the metaphysical necessity of the total structure. There is therefore no contingent total remainder requiring an external necessary ground.

## Literature status

Necessitarianism is a direct historical and contemporary metaphysical position. Spinoza is the clearest major historical anchor, although TFP does not inherit the rest of his metaphysical or theological system.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **Baruch Spinoza**, *Ethics*, Part I, especially propositions 29 and 33, traditionally read as claiming that nothing in nature is contingent and that things could not have been produced in another way or order.
2. **Don Garrett**, “Spinoza’s Necessitarianism,” in *Necessity and Nature in Spinoza's Philosophy* (Oxford University Press, 2018), pp. 98–124, defending a full necessitarian reading.
3. **Martin Lin**, “Rationalism and Necessitarianism,” *Noûs* 46 (2012): 418–448, examining the route from PSR-style rationalism to necessitarianism.

## Core truth propositions

1. The relevant totality exists metaphysically necessarily.
2. There is no metaphysically possible total alternative in which that totality fails to obtain.
3. Apparent internal contingency does not constitute a contingent total remainder.
4. No external selector or necessary ground is required to explain why this total reality rather than another obtains.

## Authority commitments

None required. Spinoza is a source, not an authority whose whole system is adopted.

## Internal success conditions

- Necessitarianism must be coherent and not simply redefine “possible” to protect actuality.
- The view must explain ordinary counterfactual and contingency discourse.
- The necessity of total reality must not be assumed merely because explanation is desired.
- It must distinguish total necessity from deterministic evolution given contingent initial conditions.
- It must not silently inherit Spinoza's substance monism unless independently argued.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

The candidate expects the apparent contingency that motivates the working question to be reducible or reinterpretable within a necessary total structure. Its primary support is modal/metaphysical, not a particular empirical signature.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Modal intuition objection:** many ordinary and scientific counterfactuals strongly suggest that things could have been otherwise.
2. **Modal-collapse objection:** rationalist principles may overgenerate necessity and erase distinctions needed for explanation.
3. **Explanatory objection:** saying totality is necessary may relocate the question to why it has that necessary nature.
4. **Determinism/necessity confusion:** causal determination within a world does not by itself show the world is metaphysically necessary.
5. **Expressive-cost objection:** the view owes an account of apparently meaningful possibility talk.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- Necessitarianism is not merely determinism.
- It does not mean humans know every necessity.
- It does not by itself imply theism, naturalism, or Spinozism.
- It does not say every proposition is logically necessary in every technical sense unless the chosen version establishes that stronger claim.
- It does not posit a second external necessary ground.

## Packet contamination note

Spinoza is used as the strongest worked historical model, but the TFP candidate is intentionally thinner than Spinoza's complete metaphysics.
