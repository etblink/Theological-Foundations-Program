# TFP-STUDY-3-CONTINGENCY — B3 Steelman Packet: C-NEC-AGENTIVE 0.1.0

## Identity

- Candidate: `C-NEC-AGENTIVE`
- Frozen label: **Necessary agentive foundation**
- Status: **FROZEN_B3_PACKET__PENDING_EQUAL_STRENGTH_REVIEW**

## Candidate-native / primary formulation

A metaphysically necessary foundation with real intentional agency or mind-like purposive capacity supplies the relevant explanation of contingent reality. Agency is explanatorily operative: contingent reality obtains because of an intentional act, reason-responsive selection, willing, or comparable purposive relation.

The family is intentionally thinner than classical theism. It does not build in perfect goodness, omniscience, omnipotence, providence, worship-worthiness, uniqueness, or identity with the God of any religion.

## Literature status

This is a direct family in contemporary contingency-argument literature. Some proponents infer further divine attributes downstream; those additional inferences are not part of the frozen Study-3 candidate.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **Timothy O’Connor**, *Theism and Ultimate Explanation: The Necessary Shape of Contingency* (Blackwell, 2008), especially the existence-stage argument for necessary being and the account of intentional explanation.
2. **Timothy O’Connor**, “Ultimate Explanation and Necessary Being,” in *Theism and Ultimate Explanation* / related published treatment (2014), emphasizing the explanatory significance of necessary being.
3. **Alexander R. Pruss and Joshua L. Rasmussen**, *Necessary Existence* (Oxford University Press, 2018), especially the chapter “An Argument from Contingency.”

These authors often defend stronger theistic conclusions than TFP permits at this stage; only the necessary-agentive explanatory core is imported into this packet.

## Core truth propositions

1. A metaphysically necessary foundation exists.
2. Intentional agency or mind-like purposive capacity is real in that foundation.
3. Agency is essential to the relevant explanation of contingent reality.
4. The foundation can stand in a truth-relevant explanatory relation to contingent reality without making that reality necessary.
5. Additional divine attributes do not follow merely from the family label.

## Authority commitments

No scriptural or ecclesial authority is required. The family may be defended in natural theology, but the Study-3 formulation is philosophical.

## Internal success conditions

- Necessary agency must be coherent.
- The account must explain how a necessary agent can produce genuinely contingent outcomes.
- Intentional explanation must add genuine explanatory content rather than merely rename the unexplained contingency.
- The agent's necessary existence must be independently warranted.
- Any move from “necessary agent” to stronger divine attributes must be separately argued and cannot be imported into this candidate.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

Candidate-native support would include a defensible contingency argument whose explanatory terminus requires purposive agency rather than an impersonal base, plus a coherent account of intentional explanation capable of terminating the relevant why-question.

No religious experience, revelation, miracle, fine-tuning result, or doctrinal datum is frozen here as evidence. G1 remains unauthorized.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Modal-collapse / freedom objection:** if the agent necessarily exists with sufficient reasons, why is the chosen contingent reality not necessary?
2. **Explanatory-gap objection:** positing an agent may shift the question to why the agent has these intentions or reasons.
3. **Agency-necessity tension:** ordinary agency is known through contingent embodied agents; the coherence of necessary agency needs argument.
4. **Impersonal-sufficiency objection:** even if a necessary foundation is warranted, agency may be an unnecessary addition.
5. **Attribute-overreach objection:** cosmological reasoning may not justify omniscience, goodness, omnipotence, uniqueness, providence, or religious identity.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- C-NEC-AGENTIVE is not equivalent to Christianity, Judaism, Islam, or classical theism.
- It does not define the agent as morally perfect.
- It does not require a temporal “first cause.”
- It does not assume creation ex nihilo as a revealed doctrine.
- It does not claim that agency automatically explains every contingent detail.
- It does not treat “necessary foundation” and “God” as synonyms.

## Packet contamination note

The packet extracts only the agentive necessary-ground structure from stronger theistic literature. All downstream God-properties remain outside the candidate unless independently warranted later.
