## Candidate-native / primary formulation

Relevant contingent reality is explained through a mutual, structural, holistic, or otherwise non-reflexive non-well-founded dependence structure. No external necessary ground is required, yet no single item explains itself. Explanatory dependence may form loops, networks, or structures that violate standard well-foundedness without thereby being vicious.

## Literature status

This family is directly anchored in contemporary grounding literature on metaphysical coherentism, circles of ground, interdependence, and non-well-founded grounding. TFP’s family is broader than any one author’s exact theory.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **Ricki Bliss**, “Viciousness and Circles of Ground,” *Metaphilosophy* 45 (2014): 245–256. Bliss argues that circles of ground are not automatically vicious and explores metaphysical coherentism.
2. *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, “Metaphysical Grounding,” for the dispute over whether grounding must be well-founded and its discussion of Bliss and related non-foundational approaches.
3. **Mark Jago**, “From Nature to Grounding,” in *Reality and its Structure* (OUP, 2018), pp. 199–216, which defends orthodox irreflexivity/asymmetry/transitivity while allowing that grounding need not be well-founded.

These sources collectively anchor non-well-founded explanation without requiring item-reflexive self-grounding.

## Core truth propositions

1. Relevant contingent reality obtains.
2. Explanation or grounding need not be well-founded in a terminal base.
3. Genuine explanatory dependence can be organized mutually, structurally, cyclically, or otherwise non-well-founded.
4. The explanatory whole is not merely an unexplained brute totality.
5. No single contingent item or totality is required to explain itself.

## Authority commitments

None beyond philosophical commitments about grounding/explanation.

## Internal success conditions

- The proposed non-well-founded structure must be intelligible.
- Mutual or cyclic dependence must perform explanatory work rather than merely redescribe co-obtainment.
- The account must identify why its circles/networks are non-vicious.
- It must preserve the frozen distinction from item-reflexive self-explanation.
- It must avoid collapsing into an infinite one-way regress or brute totality.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

Candidate-native support would come from a defensible metaphysics of explanation on which well-foundedness is not mandatory and from worked cases where interdependence or circular grounding is explanatory. The view need not predict a special empirical signature; its central burden is structural and metaphysical.

No outcome evidence is adjudicated here.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Circularity objection:** explanatory dependence may be asymmetric by nature; circles could therefore be incoherent.
2. **No-new-information objection:** mutual explanation may fail to explain because each side presupposes what it is supposed to explain.
3. **Grounding orthodoxy objection:** many theories build irreflexivity, asymmetry, transitivity, or well-foundedness into grounding.
4. **Brute-whole objection:** a network may explain internal relations while leaving the existence of the network itself brute.
5. **Collapse objection:** some loops may reduce either to a one-way regress or to item-reflexive self-explanation.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- The family does not require a thing to explain itself.
- “Circular” does not mean logically inconsistent by definition.
- The position is not just an infinite temporal regress.
- It does not claim every explanatory circle is benign.
- It does not deny all fundamentality; it denies that explanatory adequacy necessarily requires a well-founded external terminus.
