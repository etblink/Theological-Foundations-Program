## 1. Purpose

TFP seeks to determine which theological claims or frameworks, **if any**, are true—or closest to the truth that the evidence and arguments warrant.

This protocol governs the bounded path:

```text
question
→ preregistration
→ candidate construction
→ evidence acquisition
→ typed lane analysis
→ comparative synthesis
→ adversarial audit
→ human acceptance
→ canonical bounded adjudication
```

The procedure must be able both to:
- license a bounded truth judgment when warranted;
- and withhold such a judgment when the evidence cannot carry it.

# G0 — QUESTION AUTHORIZATION

## 4. Phase A — Truth-question charter

Before evidence acquisition, freeze the exact question.

The charter MUST state:

### A1. Exact truth question
Use an adjudicable proposition or comparison.

### A2. Scope
State:
- time period;
- source corpus;
- traditions/candidates initially in view;
- geographic and linguistic scope where relevant;
- downstream claims excluded;
- scope limits that make the result bounded.

### A3. Claim types
State the proposed types for every material subclaim.

### A4. Dependency / link structure
Decompose multi-step reasoning into nodes and arrows.

Example:

```text
text says X
→ X is historically early
→ X reflects belief Y
→ Y was transmitted continuously
→ Y is doctrinally authoritative
→ Y is true
```

Each arrow is separately testable.

### A5. Initial candidate universe and major alternatives
Freeze the admitted candidate set and known serious alternatives.

### A6. Expected evidence and weakening evidence
For each candidate, state:
- evidence expected if true;
- evidence compatible but non-discriminating;
- evidence that would weaken it;
- evidence that would contradict a necessary claim where such evidence is possible.

### A7. Preregistered discriminators
Freeze the candidate-distinguishing observations or arguments **before evidence acquisition**.

### A8. Background-assumption register
Freeze relevant background assumptions, including:
- metaphysical;
- historiographic;
- epistemological;
- linguistic;
- statistical/empirical;
- confessional or anti-confessional.

No naturalism, supernaturalism, confessional authority, or skepticism may be silently installed as background.

### A9. Stop conditions
State what would count as:
- sufficient coverage;
- `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL`;
- `UNDERDETERMINED`;
- dependence on unavailable evidence.

### A10. Reopen conditions
State what new evidence, argument, defeater, dating change, source change, or audit result would justify reopening.

### A11. Audit criteria
Freeze the standard audit checklist plus any study-specific risk checks.

### A12. Authorization act
Record who authorized the bounded study and the exact authorized scope.

No study proceeds to G1 without a recorded G0 authorization.

## 5. Phase B — Candidate universe

### B1. One symmetric admission rule

A candidate may be admitted regardless of whether it comes from tradition, scholarship, skepticism, philosophy, or evidence-generated reconstruction only if it is:

1. relevant to the frozen truth question;
2. materially distinct in at least one truth-relevant proposition;
3. specified enough to generate testable evidential, logical, historical, or explanatory consequences;
4. not already subsumed by an admitted candidate;
5. supportable by a traceable formulation rather than a caricature.

The same specification burden applies to inherited, skeptical, hybrid, minority, and newly generated candidates.

### B2. Candidate elicitation

Before candidate closure:
- conduct a neutral literature/tradition survey;
- require at least one independent candidate-elicitation pass by a researcher/auditor who did not construct the initial list;
- explicitly ask for minority, hybrid, revised, and cross-tradition candidates.

### B3. Candidate exclusions

An exclusion MUST state:
- candidate;
- reason;
- evidence;
- whether exclusion is substantive or merely out-of-scope.

Exclusion because a candidate depends on an already rejected assumption is valid only if that assumption was rejected by a prior canonical bounded adjudication applicable to the present scope.

Potentially outcome-determinative exclusions require independent review before G1.

### B4. Completeness gate

Candidate closure passes only when:

- major historically/scholarly represented candidate classes have been searched;
- independent elicitation finds no omitted material candidate, or omitted candidates are explicitly reviewed;
- hybrid/revised possibilities have been considered;
- the register states why the field is sufficient **for this bounded scope**.

Completeness never means metaphysical certainty that no unknown candidate exists.

### B5. Post-freeze candidate additions

A later-discovered candidate is labeled `LATE_CANDIDATE`.

If it could materially change the result:
- pause comparative synthesis;
- amend the candidate register;
- log why it was missed;
- update discriminators where necessary;
- require an independent amendment review;
- if post-evidence knowledge shaped the new discriminator, that discriminator is exploratory and cannot alone ground canonical adjudication until a preregistered follow-up.

If immaterial, record why.

### B6. Meta-outcomes are not candidates

`NONE_ADEQUATE_WITHIN_SCOPE`, `UNDERDETERMINED_WITHIN_SCOPE`, and `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL_WITHIN_SCOPE` are outcomes, not candidate frameworks.

## 6. Phase C — Claim typing

Claim types include:

- textual;
- historical;
- archaeological/material;
- linguistic;
- interpretive;
- philosophical;
- metaphysical;
- doctrinal;
- empirical;
- experiential/testimonial;
- psychological/sociological explanatory;
- normative/moral;
- revelation;
- authority/canon;
- faith commitment.

A claim may have multiple types.

### C1. Typing freeze

Initial typing is frozen at G0 before evidence acquisition.

### C2. Retyping

Post-freeze retyping requires:
- old type;
- new type;
- reason;
- changed evidence burden;
- date;
- independent review if the retyping could materially affect the outcome.

Retyping may not be used to lower an evidential burden after contrary evidence appears.

### C3. Multi-type conflict rule

When a claim spans types:
- each type-specific component receives its own disposition;
- conflicting components remain visible;
- the claim receives no stronger overall disposition than its weakest **necessary** truth-bearing component unless the preregistration explicitly shows why that component is non-necessary.

### C4. Faith commitments

A faith commitment may be recorded as:
`CONFESSIONAL_COMMITMENT`.

It is not public evidence by itself and cannot raise a public truth adjudication.

If a faith commitment is translated into historical, philosophical, revelation, authority, or other adjudicable premises, those premises are evaluated under their ordinary burdens.

---

## 7. Phase D — Link decomposition

Every multi-step inference must identify:

- nodes: propositions;
- arrows: inferential or historical transitions;
- necessary vs optional links;
- evidence bearing on each link;
- alternative paths;
- failure consequences.

A failure of one arrow does not automatically falsify unrelated nodes.

Long theological narratives may not be adjudicated as indivisible stories.

# G1 — EVIDENCE ACQUISITION

## 8. Phase E — Source and evidence acquisition

Every study must preserve an acquisition log.

### E1. Search / source plan
Before searching, state:
- databases / corpora / repositories / canonical sources to be searched;
- primary and secondary source classes;
- languages/editions where material;
- date cut or literature window where needed;
- search terms or source-selection rules;
- inclusion and exclusion criteria.

### E2. Evidence provenance
Record, as relevant:
- manuscript/source identity;
- edition/translation;
- date and dating uncertainty;
- authorship/provenance;
- archaeological context;
- chain of custody;
- analytical method;
- publication status;
- quotation context;
- dependency relationships;
- philosophical argument source.

### E3. Negative and inaccessible evidence
Record:
- unsuccessful searches;
- expected but absent evidence where absence is probative;
- inaccessible sources;
- known missing data;
- reasons access failed.

### E4. Coverage state
Coverage is classified:
- `COVERAGE_COMPLETE_FOR_FROZEN_SCOPE`;
- `COVERAGE_MATERIALLY_COMPLETE_WITH_LISTED_GAPS`;
- `COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE`.

Only the first two may support canonical comparative adjudication.

`COVERAGE_INCOMPLETE` normally yields `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL_WITHIN_SCOPE` unless the missing material is demonstrably irrelevant to the conclusion.

# G2 — TYPE-SPECIFIC LANE SYNTHESIS

## 9. Phase F — Proposition-level dispositions

All consequential proposition dispositions include the suffix `_WITHIN_SCOPE`.

### F1. `SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use only when:
- the claim-type sufficiency conditions are met;
- provenance/quality is adequate;
- serious rival interpretations/explanations relevant to the proposition have been tested;
- no undefeated critical defeater remains.

### F2. `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- at least one material component is supported;
- but one or more necessary components remain weak, unresolved, or dependent on a major live assumption.

### F3. `PLAUSIBLE_BUT_UNATTESTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- the proposition is coherent/possible within frozen background;
- but the evidence required to show it actually occurred or is instantiated is absent.

### F4. `NOT_ESTABLISHED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- available evidence does not meet the minimum support burden;
- but the evidence does not materially favor the negation or rival.

This is not evidence that the claim is false.

### F5. `EVIDENCE_AGAINST_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- material evidence is less expected if the proposition were true than under a serious rival/negation;
- or an undefeated major defeater exists;
- but contradiction is not decisive.

### F6. `CONTRADICTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use only when:
- a necessary component conflicts with high-quality evidence or valid argument under the frozen scope/background;
- and no live repair consistent with the candidate's frozen formulation remains.

### F7. `UNDERDETERMINED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- evidence coverage is adequate;
- at least two materially distinct candidates remain live;
- available evidence/argument cannot discriminate them enough to justify preference.

Subtype MUST be recorded:
- `EVIDENTIAL_EQUIVALENCE`;
- `FRAMEWORK_DEPENDENCE`;
- `PHILOSOPHICAL_EQUIVALENCE`;
- `MIXED_TRADEOFF`.

### F8. `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- evidence quality/coverage is too weak or incomplete to compare candidates responsibly.

Subtype MUST be recorded:
- `MISSING_CRITICAL_EVIDENCE`;
- `PROVENANCE_FAILURE`;
- `SOURCE_ACCESS_FAILURE`;
- `QUESTION_NOT_OPERATIONALIZED`.

### F9. No counting rule
Dispositions are not votes.
Repeated dependent evidence does not gain weight by repetition.

## 10. Phase G — Type-specific minimum sufficiency

The following are minimum contents for `SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`.

They are necessary templates, not automatic sufficient conditions.

### Textual
Require:
- stable enough textual base for the question;
- material variants identified;
- grammar/syntax/genre/context assessed;
- serious rival readings compared;
- later doctrinal usage not substituted for the earlier text.

Earlier manuscript evidence is not automatically better if less reliable.

### Historical
Require:
- provenance/dating/genre/interests assessed;
- literary or informational dependence assessed;
- contextual plausibility;
- expected evidence and significant absences considered;
- serious alternative explanations tested;
- later testimony discounted only according to actual dependence/distance/reliability, not by age alone.

### Archaeological/material
Require:
- context/provenance;
- dating;
- analytical method;
- relevant controls/comparators;
- alternative functional interpretations;
- no unacknowledged chain-of-custody problem.

### Linguistic
Require:
- corpus at relevant historical stage;
- semantic range;
- syntax/context;
- diachronic change;
- cognates/comparanda where relevant;
- no fixed-definition fallacy.

### Interpretive
Require:
- secure textual/material base;
- local and broader context;
- explanatory coverage;
- rival readings;
- non-circularity;
- clear distinction between original meaning and later reception.

### Philosophical
Require:
- explicit argument form;
- validity or identified inductive/abductive structure;
- premise warrant;
- hidden-premise search;
- major defeaters;
- serious rival arguments/frameworks;
- sensitivity to changes in disputed premises.

### Metaphysical
Require:
- defined ontology/modal commitments;
- coherence;
- argument for central commitments;
- rival ontologies;
- defeaters/counterexamples;
- distinction between conceivability, possibility, necessity, and actuality.

### Doctrinal
Require:
- exact doctrinal proposition;
- internal implications/coherence;
- authority/source derivation;
- historical development where relevant.

**Doctrinal sources establish what a system teaches, not automatically whether it is true.**
A doctrinal truth verdict must inherit separately adjudicated support from its necessary textual, historical, philosophical, revelation, authority, normative, or other truth-bearing premises.

### Empirical
Require:
- operationalized variables;
- appropriate method;
- data quality;
- robustness/replication proportionate to claim;
- alternative mechanisms;
- uncertainty.

A non-observational claim should not be forced into this type.

### Experiential/testimonial
Require:
- authenticity of testimony;
- opportunity/access;
- reliability factors;
- independence/dependence;
- transmission distortion;
- alternative psychological/social explanations;
- external corroboration proportionate to any external-world claim.

Sincerity alone does not establish external truth.

### Psychological/sociological explanatory
Require:
- construct validity;
- population/sample relevance;
- causal or mechanistic evidence proportionate to the claim;
- competing mechanisms;
- avoidance of pathologizing or genetic-fallacy inference.

### Normative/moral
Require:
- explicit normative proposition;
- metaethical background;
- consistency;
- counterexamples;
- practical implications;
- competing normative accounts;
- distinction between descriptive origin and normative warrant.

### Revelation
Require the dedicated procedure in Phase I.

### Authority/canon
Require:
- exact authority claim;
- historical basis;
- transmission/canonicalization evidence;
- non-circular warrant for the authority relation;
- rival authority claims;
- scope of authority.

### Faith commitment
Cannot by itself receive `SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE` as a publicly adjudicated truth claim.
It may be preserved as `CONFESSIONAL_COMMITMENT`.

For weaker dispositions, the researcher must state which minimum components failed or remain unresolved.

## 11. Phase H — Philosophical, metaphysical, and normative evaluation

For argument-heavy lanes, record:

1. proposition;
2. argument form;
3. premise list;
4. premise-warrant source;
5. inferential validity/strength;
6. hidden assumptions;
7. major defeaters;
8. rival arguments/frameworks;
9. theoretical virtues, treated as defeasible rather than decisive:
   - coherence;
   - explanatory scope;
   - explanatory depth;
   - parsimony;
   - unification;
   - fit with other warranted beliefs;
10. sensitivity:
   - which premise change flips the result?
   - which defeater would defeat the argument?

Parsimony is one defeasible virtue.
"Fewest assumptions" is not an automatic winner.

## 12. Phase I — Miracle and revelation procedure

### I1. Symmetric starting rule

Do NOT assume:
- naturalism;
- supernaturalism;
- that sincere testimony is sufficient;
- that miracles are impossible by definition;
- that extraordinary claims are true because alternatives are incomplete.

"Ordinary historical standards" means source criticism, provenance, dating, dependence, context, testimony evaluation, alternative explanation, and expected-evidence analysis **without metaphysical closure built in**.

### I2. Miracle/anomalous-event sequence

Evaluate separately:

1. **Report claim:** was a miracle/anomaly report made?
2. **Transmission claim:** how reliably was the report preserved?
3. **Historical-core claim:** what event/experience is best established?
4. **Anomaly claim:** does the established event exceed the ordinary explanation currently warranted?
5. **Causal-class comparison:** compare live classes:
   - ordinary natural;
   - rare natural;
   - psychological/social;
   - fraud/error;
   - unknown;
   - non-natural candidate where philosophically admissible.
6. **Metaphysical admissibility:** what background assumptions make each class possible/impossible?
7. **Particular-cause identification:** if non-natural causation remains warranted, what evidence identifies a particular agent/cause?
8. **Theological consequence:** what downstream theological proposition, if any, actually follows?

No step may be skipped.

### I3. Explanation comparison without hidden priors

For each explanation, record:
- what evidence it predicts/expects;
- what evidence is surprising under it;
- auxiliary assumptions required;
- independently warranted background commitments;
- unresolved anomalies;
- defeaters.

If a probabilistic prior is used, its basis must be explicit and sensitivity-tested.
If no defensible prior can be specified, do not hide one inside words such as "ordinary" or "extraordinary"; use `FRAMEWORK_DEPENDENCE` or another underdetermination subtype where appropriate.

### I4. Revelation sequence

Evaluate separately:

1. a revelation claim was made;
2. claimant sincerity/experience;
3. historical transmission of the claim;
4. philosophical possibility of revelation;
5. evidential markers that revelation rather than ordinary cognition/explanation occurred;
6. identification of the purported source;
7. warrant for treating the source as authoritative/truthful;
8. preservation of the revealed content;
9. doctrinal consequences.

Competing revelation claims from different traditions face the same sequence.

Revelation content cannot authenticate its own authority solely by circular appeal unless a separate argument for self-authentication is explicitly defended and audited.

## 13. Phase J — Source-quality and independence rules

There is **no universal lexical source hierarchy**.

Evaluate each source on question-relevant dimensions:

- proximity;
- reliability;
- genre;
- access to events/data;
- independence;
- transmission history;
- bias/interests;
- preservation;
- corroboration;
- provenance.

Rules:

1. Earlier ≠ automatically better.
2. Later ≠ automatically worse.
3. A later source may preserve earlier material if dependence/transmission evidence supports that claim.
4. An early source may be unreliable.
5. "Independent" means no material common source, literary dependence, common informant, coordinated institution, or shared evidential bottleneck relevant to the claimed convergence. Degrees of dependence must be recorded.
6. Mixed questions use claim-by-claim source evaluation; no single hierarchy governs every claim type.
7. Numbered source lists in study plans are workflow order, not evidential rank unless explicitly stated.

## 14. Phase K — Discriminators

### K1. Freeze point
Primary discriminators MUST be registered in G0 before evidence acquisition.

### K2. Required contents
Each discriminator records:
- candidates compared;
- observation/argument;
- why it discriminates;
- background assumptions;
- decision relevance:
  - `CRITICAL`: bears on a necessary proposition or directly entails/excludes a candidate;
  - `MATERIAL`: meaningfully changes relative warrant;
  - `CONTEXTUAL`: relevant but non-decisive.

These are priority classes, not numerical weights.

### K3. Unsupported assumption
"Unsupported" means unsupported **relative to the frozen background register and evidence available within scope**.
Disagreement about background becomes an explicit background-assumption dispute, not an invisible penalty.

### K4. Post-evidence discriminator
A discriminator discovered after evidence acquisition is labeled `POST_EVIDENCE_EXPLORATORY`.
It may guide follow-up work but cannot be the sole basis of canonical adjudication until preregistered in a fresh bounded follow-up or independently validated in a way specified at G0.

### K5. No tallying
Do not count "wins."
One CRITICAL discriminator may dominate many merely contextual compatibilities.

If discriminators point in irreducible different directions without a principled priority rule, use `MIXED_TRADEOFF` underdetermination.

## 15. Phase L — Evidence lanes and lane freeze

### L1. Lane construction
Define lanes by claim competence, not by preferred candidate.

Examples:
- textual;
- historical;
- philosophical;
- doctrinal;
- empirical;
- experiential;
- normative;
- revelation/authority.

Do not label a rival explanation lane "alternative" if that implies a default candidate.

### L2. Lane independence
A lane may use another lane's **frozen evidential finding** but may not inherit its adjudicative conclusion.

Use the term `FROZEN_FINDING`, not "accepted evidence."

### L3. Background assumptions
Every lane receives the same frozen background-assumption register plus any lane-specific additions, which must be logged.

A philosophical assumption that changes a historical inference must be cited at the exact dependency point.

### L4. Lane freeze
A lane is frozen only when:
- scope is complete;
- coverage state is recorded;
- proposition dispositions are issued;
- unresolved conflicts are listed;
- artifact path/version/commit is fixed;
- no further edits occur except through a versioned amendment.

### L5. Cross-lane conflicts
Conflicts remain explicit.
No lane may harmonize another lane silently.

### L6. Cumulative-case synthesis
Cross-lane synthesis may begin only after all required lanes are frozen.

The synthesis uses a dependency graph:
- which lane finding supports which proposition;
- whether evidence streams are independent or dependent;
- whether a claim requires conjunction of multiple lanes;
- whether one lane supplies a defeater to another.

Cumulative force comes from convergent independent support for the **same necessary proposition**, not from counting favorable lanes.

## 16. Phase M — Historical continuity relation taxonomy

C0–C7 are **relation types**, not a universal linear ladder and not mandatory sequential rungs.

- **C0 — recurrence:** similar motif/proposition appears in two contexts.
- **C1 — form/material continuity:** related form/artifact/textual formula can be chronologically traced.
- **C2 — carrier continuity:** plausible historical carrier such as person, community, institution, text, trade, apprenticeship, or transmission chain is evidenced.
- **C3 — practice continuity:** comparable ritual/institutional/practical use persists.
- **C4 — semantic continuity:** demonstrably comparable conceptual work persists.
- **C5 — named textual continuity:** explicit text names the concept/deity/proposition/relation.
- **C6 — genealogical continuity:** evidence supports historical derivation rather than mere similarity or independent reinvention.
- **C7 — doctrinal continuity:** a later doctrine preserves, intentionally develops, or explicitly recovers an earlier truth-relevant proposition with enough textual/historical evidence that the connection is more than thematic resemblance.

Possible patterns include:
- branching;
- convergence;
- loss;
- recovery;
- refunctionalization;
- parallel independent construction;
- discontinuity.

### M1. Symmetric burden
Claims of continuity and claims of discontinuity/corruption both require evidence.

Absence of C-level evidence does not itself establish discontinuity.

### M2. Independent reinvention
Use independent reinvention as a live null when common conditions could plausibly generate similar forms, but it is not automatically preferred.
Transmission and independent invention are compared under equal evidential burdens.

### M3. Relevance to truth
Historical development may legitimately bear on truth when a candidate's warrant depends on:
- apostolic/original authorship;
- faithful transmission;
- continuity of revelation;
- institutional succession;
- original intended meaning;
- or another historically contingent authority claim.

Historical origin does not automatically falsify a proposition whose truth does not depend on that origin.

Continuity itself does not prove the proposition true.

# G3 — COMPARATIVE ADJUDICATION

## 17. Phase N — Cross-lane inferential bridge

This phase turns frozen proposition findings into a provisional bounded study outcome.

### N1. Critical-proposition map
For each candidate, identify:
- **necessary truth-bearing propositions**;
- supporting but non-necessary propositions;
- explicit candidate dependencies.

These classifications must have been frozen at G0 or amended transparently.

### N2. Defeater-first priority
Apply in this order:

1. A necessary proposition `CONTRADICTED_WITHIN_SCOPE` prevents the candidate from receiving a truth-warranted outcome within that scope.
2. A necessary proposition `EVIDENCE_AGAINST_WITHIN_SCOPE` is a major live defeater and must be explicitly overcome; favorable contextual evidence cannot simply outvote it.
3. A necessary proposition `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL_WITHIN_SCOPE` blocks truth adjudication.
4. A necessary proposition `NOT_ESTABLISHED_WITHIN_SCOPE` blocks `TRUTH_WARRANTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`, though the candidate may still be best supported relative to weaker rivals.
5. `PARTIALLY_SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE` on a necessary proposition allows relative comparison but normally blocks a truth-warranted outcome unless the unsupported component is explicitly non-essential to the exact truth claim.
6. Independent, direct, CRITICAL discriminatory evidence outranks generic compatibility or repeated dependent evidence.
7. Internal coherence never substitutes for external warrant.

### N3. Candidate dominance without scoring
Candidate A **dominates** Candidate B within scope only if:
- no predesignated CRITICAL proposition/discriminator materially favors B over A without resolution;
- A is at least as well warranted as B on every truth-critical comparison that can be made;
- and at least one truth-critical comparison materially favors A.

If neither dominates because of irreducible tradeoffs, use `UNDERDETERMINED_WITHIN_SCOPE: MIXED_TRADEOFF`.

No numerical or unweighted win/loss tally is permitted.

### N4. Internal adequacy
A candidate is internally adequate only if:
- it is sufficiently specified;
- no internal contradiction defeats it;
- it satisfies its own necessary explanatory/doctrinal constraints.

### N5. External warrant
Evaluate the truth-bearing premises themselves under the appropriate claim-type rules.

A candidate cannot become truth-warranted solely from internal adequacy.

## 18. Phase N2 — Study-level outcomes

Every outcome contains `WITHIN_SCOPE`.

### `CANDIDATE_A_BEST_SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- candidate universe gate passed;
- A dominates or is better warranted than every serious admitted rival on the frozen critical comparisons;
- but one or more necessary truth-bearing propositions do not meet the threshold for `TRUTH_WARRANTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`.

This is **not** a truth verdict.

### `CANDIDATE_A_TRUTH_WARRANTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use only when ALL are true:

1. the exact proposition claimed true is explicit;
2. candidate-universe completeness gate passed;
3. all necessary truth-bearing propositions are `SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
4. no undefeated CRITICAL or MAJOR defeater remains;
5. serious rival candidates have been compared under equal standards;
6. the result rests on truth-relevant external warrant, not merely internal coherence, historical priority, consensus, or tradition;
7. background assumptions necessary to the conclusion are themselves supported or sensitivity-tested;
8. evidence coverage is complete or materially complete for the frozen scope;
9. independent audit returns `PASS` or `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`;
10. the human owner explicitly accepts the bounded truth adjudication in a separate acceptance record.

"Truth warranted" means TFP is warranted to assert the proposition true **within the frozen scope, evidence state, and stated background assumptions**.
It is not infallibility or metaphysical certainty.

### `CANDIDATE_A_CLOSEST_TO_TRUTH_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use only when:
- no candidate qualifies as truth-warranted;
- candidate universe gate passed;
- A dominates each serious rival on the frozen truth-critical comparisons;
- A has no necessary proposition contradicted within scope;
- residual unsupported/partially supported components are explicitly identified;
- the sense in which A is "closer" is stated proposition-by-proposition, not as a global score;
- independent audit and human acceptance occur.

### `REVISED_CANDIDATE_REQUIRED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when:
- independently supported components survive;
- but no admitted candidate adequately combines them.

This does not automatically create a canonical new candidate.
It triggers a new preregistered candidate-construction cycle.

### `NONE_ADEQUATE_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when every admitted candidate fails the preregistered minimum adequacy gate through contradiction, incoherence, or a critical unresolved failure.

Do not use this merely because evidence is sparse.

### `UNDERDETERMINED_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when two or more adequate candidates remain and evidence/argument cannot warrant a preference.

Record subtype.

### `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL_WITHIN_SCOPE`
Use when evidence coverage/quality is too weak for responsible comparison.

Record subtype.

## 19. Origins / development / meaning / truth

Every relevant study must separately record:

- **Origins** — how the claim/practice arose;
- **Development** — how it changed/transmitted/institutionalized;
- **Meaning** — what it meant in the context being studied;
- **Truth** — whether the truth-bearing proposition is warranted.

Rules:

1. Natural origin ≠ falsity.
2. Ancient origin ≠ truth.
3. Later development ≠ corruption by default.
4. Later development ≠ legitimate maturation by default.
5. Development evidence bears directly on truth **when the truth claim includes historical continuity, original revelation, authority, succession, faithful preservation, or original meaning as a necessary premise**.
6. Otherwise development may affect evidential context without deciding the proposition's truth.

# G4 — ADVERSARIAL AUDIT

## 20. Phase O — Mandatory independent audit

Any study proposing:
- a canonical theological adjudication;
- `TRUTH_WARRANTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
- `CLOSEST_TO_TRUTH_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
- or a bounded result likely to alter program-level truth commitments

MUST receive an independent audit.

### O1. Independence minimum
The sole independent auditor:
- may not be the author of the comparative synthesis;
- may not be the sole author of any outcome-determinative lane;
- must receive frozen artifacts;
- must not modify the study while auditing.

For high-consequence program-level commitments, the human owner may require multiple independent auditors.

### O2. Frozen audit criteria
The standard audit checks are fixed by this protocol and any study-specific additions are frozen at G0.

Audit MUST test:
- governance compliance;
- candidate completeness/exclusions;
- claim-typing drift;
- discriminator timing;
- source-selection bias;
- lane leakage;
- background assumptions;
- premise warrant;
- equal-standard application;
- shallow-heuristic reproduction;
- outcome-scope discipline;
- uncertainty completeness;
- cold-start reproducibility.

### O3. Blinding
Use blinding when it reduces a real bias.

At minimum, where practical:
- auditor should evaluate frozen reasoning before reading any human-owner preference;
- tradition labels may be withheld in semantic sub-audits where identity is not necessary;
- provisional acceptance must not be shown as authoritative fact.

Do not blind away context necessary for valid interpretation.

### O4. Audit outcomes
- `PASS`
- `PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS`
- `REPAIR_REQUIRED`
- `INVALID_COMPARISON`
- `INSUFFICIENT_SIGNAL`

### O5. Auditor disagreement
If multiple independent auditors materially disagree:
- status becomes `DISPUTED_AUDIT`;
- no canonical theological adjudication may be accepted until:
  - the disagreement is reconciled by a frozen response/re-audit, or
  - the human owner records an explicit decision and rationale after reviewing both reports.

### O6. Repair and re-audit
After `REPAIR_REQUIRED`:
- freeze a repair matrix before editing;
- preserve the original report;
- version the repaired artifacts;
- run a focused independent re-audit against frozen repair criteria;
- the repair author may not self-certify.

### O7. PASS_WITH_LIMITATIONS
Every limitation must:
- appear in the acceptance record;
- be reflected in canonical STATE if accepted;
- constrain downstream use.

Audit PASS never equals canonical acceptance.

# G5 — HUMAN ACCEPTANCE

## 21. Phase P — Acceptance authority

### P1. Procedural gate completion
Authorized research agents/program lead may record:
- evidence acquisition complete;
- lane freeze;
- audit completion;
- repair completion;
- non-truth procedural state transitions

within already authorized scope.

### P2. Canonical theological adjudication
**Every canonical theological adjudication requires explicit human-owner acceptance.**

This includes:
- `CANDIDATE_A_BEST_SUPPORTED_WITHIN_SCOPE` if it is to become canonical program knowledge;
- `CANDIDATE_A_TRUTH_WARRANTED_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
- `CANDIDATE_A_CLOSEST_TO_TRUTH_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
- `NONE_ADEQUATE_WITHIN_SCOPE`;
- `UNDERDETERMINED_WITHIN_SCOPE` when canonically closing a major truth question.

The independent auditor cannot perform acceptance.
The synthesis author cannot substitute for acceptance.
A STATE update cannot substitute for acceptance.

### P3. Acceptance record
Canonical acceptance requires a separate versioned artifact containing:

- study ID/version;
- exact bounded outcome;
- exact scope;
- synthesis artifact;
- audit artifact;
- audit limitations;
- uncertainty statement;
- negative knowledge;
- reopen conditions;
- explicit human-owner acceptance statement;
- date.

### P4. Program-level truth commitments
Statements such as:
- "Christianity is true";
- "Tradition X is the true church";
- "No religion is true"

are program-level commitments and always require:
- explicit human-owner authorization;
- evidence from multiple appropriately scoped bounded adjudications;
- a separate program-level synthesis/audit/acceptance process.

They may never be inferred automatically from one bounded study.

### P5. Canonical STATE entry
A canonical truth-bearing STATE entry MUST cite:
- bounded adjudication record;
- independent audit;
- separate human-owner acceptance record.

Without all three, the entry is non-canonical.

# G6 — UNCERTAINTY, STOP, HOLD, REOPEN

## 22. Phase Q — Mandatory uncertainty statement

Every comparative synthesis and canonical adjudication MUST state:

1. **scope:** exact domain in which the result applies;
2. **confidence in disposition:** `HIGH`, `MODERATE`, or `LOW`, with rationale;
3. **evidence coverage state;**
4. **named residual live alternatives;**
5. **key background assumptions;**
6. **known weaknesses;**
7. **missing/inaccessible evidence;**
8. **what specific evidence/argument would flip or materially weaken the result;**
9. **underdetermination subtype**, if applicable;
10. **audit limitations**, if any.

Confidence and scope are separate.
A narrow result may have high confidence.
A broad result may have low confidence.

---

## 23. Phase Q2 — Stop and reopen

### Q2.1 Coverage-based stop test
A lane/study may stop when:

- the preregistered source/search strata have been completed or explicitly documented as inaccessible;
- all known outcome-material sources are addressed or listed as unresolved;
- additional accessible material identified in the bounded search is predominantly dependent/repetitive or not expected to alter a truth-critical proposition;
- remaining gaps and their possible effect are stated;
- audit can inspect the coverage record.

"Low expected information value" alone is not sufficient without this coverage record.

### Q2.2 Mandatory G6 state
Every stop maps to exactly one:
- `CLOSED`;
- `HELD`;
- `SUPERSEDED`;
- `REOPENED`.

### Q2.3 Mandatory reopen_if
Every `CLOSED` or `HELD` study MUST define `reopen_if`.

Possible reopen triggers include:
- new evidence;
- new manuscript/source;
- changed dating/provenance;
- independent replication;
- new philosophical argument;
- new defeater;
- changed premise warrant;
- credible audit challenge;
- candidate-universe expansion materially relevant to the conclusion.

### Q2.4 Endless-literature control
A study is not required to absorb indefinitely growing literature if:
- the bounded coverage plan is complete;
- later material is tracked for materially new evidence/argument;
- and reopen conditions capture result-changing developments.

## 24. Anti-heuristic safeguard

Neither researcher nor auditor may mechanically apply:

- always prefer earliest source;
- always prefer scholarly consensus;
- always distrust later doctrine;
- always trust established tradition;
- always choose the simplest explanation;
- always prefer the interpretation with the fewest assumptions;
- always choose naturalistic explanation;
- always choose supernatural explanation;
- always reject miracle testimony;
- always accept sincere miracle testimony;
- always treat doctrinal development as corruption;
- always treat doctrinal development as legitimate maturation;
- always prefer independent invention;
- always prefer transmission.

The independent audit MUST ask whether one such heuristic could reproduce the outcome without integrating the evidence.

If yes, the study fails the anti-heuristic check unless the rule itself was independently justified for that exact question.

## 25. Cold-start reproducibility check

Before G5 acceptance, an unfamiliar competent researcher must be able to recover from frozen artifacts:

- authorization;
- question;
- candidates/exclusions;
- claim types and any retyping;
- evidence plan and coverage;
- background assumptions;
- discriminators and timing;
- lane findings;
- cross-lane dependencies;
- proposition dispositions;
- outcome rule;
- uncertainty;
- audit result;
- acceptance authority;
- stop/reopen state.

If any decisive inference depends on undocumented project lore, the study cannot be canonically accepted.
