## A. Candidate elicitation

Candidates are defined by what happened. Q2's causal classes are a separate dimension (see the end of this section), not candidates.

**R — Embodied post-death living state, causally originating the proclamation** (full candidate)
- **Strongest formulation:**
  - Jesus died on the cross.
  - After death he existed again in a genuinely embodied living state.
  - Encounters with that embodied person were a material cause of the earliest resurrection proclamation and experience reports.
- **Core propositions:**
  - death on the cross;
  - post-mortem embodied existence;
  - real encounters with that embodied referent;
  - those encounters causing the proclamation.
- **Variants (submodels), differing on a necessary proposition:**
  - R-phys: physical continuity and an empty tomb.
  - R-trans: a transformed or "pneumatic" body, tomb-agnostic.
- **Distinctness:** the only family on which Q1 is true.
- **Anti-narrowing note:** the core must be written at the weakest shared level, and each variant keeps its own necessary propositions. Otherwise a flexible formulation can drop the tomb as "non-necessary" when the tomb is its most vulnerable claim.

**V — Veridical but non-embodied post-mortem encounters** (full candidate)
- **Formulation:**
  - Jesus died.
  - Real extramental encounter experiences followed, caused by Jesus surviving death in a non-embodied mode (exaltation, heavenly or spiritual presence).
  - He had no embodied living state in the world.
- **Distinct on:** the ontology of the referent. This is the candidate that fits sources read as "spiritual resurrection" or "exaltation with visions".
- **Variants:** theistic (God-given vision or exaltation) and non-theistic survival, including a parapsychological variant.

**H — Naturalistic experiential-psychological origin** (full candidate)
- **Formulation:**
  - Jesus died.
  - Followers had genuine subjective experiences without a veridical referent: grief or bereavement apparitions, ecstatic or visionary states, group dynamics, cognitive dissonance.
  - Those experiences generated the proclamation.
- **Variants:** individual vs collective mechanisms, plus a separate account of Paul's experience.
- **Distinct from L:** H holds that experiences really occurred and caused belief.

**L — Interpretive and legendary development origin** (full candidate)
- **Formulation:**
  - The proclamation arose from exaltation and vindication theology, scriptural exegesis and community narrative.
  - Appearance and tomb traditions are secondary, constructed or developed. There is little or no experiential seed at the origin.
- **Distinct on:** the necessary proposition that early experiences are not the primary cause.
- **Variant:** body-disposal or non-burial models, where the tomb tradition is secondary legend.

**S — Non-death or substitution** (full candidate family)
- **Why it's needed:** Q1 itself names survival without death as a rival.
- **Variants, differing on necessary propositions and causal class:**
  - S1: natural survival or rescue (swoon).
  - S2: substitution or misidentified victim.
  - S3: divine rescue without death (the Islamic rafʿ-type model).
- S2 and S3 should be submodels if S1 carries the family.

**F — Deliberate deception** (admit as a candidate, or as a named rival explanation for specific propositions)
- **Formulation:** body theft or deliberate fabrication by leaders.
- **Standing:** it has ancient traditional representation (the guard narrative, Justin, Tertullian) and is distinct on the necessary proposition "intentional fabrication". It has little modern scholarly defense.
- **Recommendation:** exclusion risks the "narrowed field" objection, so admit it unless an independent reviewer shows it subsumed by composite modules.

**C — Composite or multicausal naturalistic** (admit only under a preregistered module rule)
- **What it is:** a mix such as H for Paul, L for the tomb, and misidentification for some episodes.
- **Anti-post-hoc rule:** before evidence exposure, freeze:
  - an enumerated module list;
  - a map from each necessary proposition or evidence class to exactly one module;
  - each module's independent warrant for its mechanism;
  - a ban on adding or reassigning modules after exposure.
- The candidate must be defined by this map, not by "whatever fits".
- Any later module addition routes through C4 amendment control.

- Other non-divine agents (angelic, demonic, spirit).
- Rare or undiagnosed natural process.

## B. Exclusion risks

**Probably excludable**
- Christianity as a whole, the Trinity, atonement, inerrancy and later doctrine: outside scope.
- "God exists" as a free-standing candidate: not a Q1 comparator.
- Space-alien, time-travel and simulation models: no serious representation or independent argument.
- "Whatever explains the evidence": barred by the prompt.
- Wholesale later-miracle claims: out of scope.

**Exclusion risks that could artificially narrow the field**
1. **Non-historicity (mythicist) models.**
   - Q1 presupposes "Jesus of Nazareth". These models have minority but real academic representation.
   - Excluding them is a substantive scope decision. Treat them as a node-level rival on the "existed and was crucified" proposition rather than a full candidate. Record the exclusion reason and have it independently reviewed.
2. **S2 and S3**, if S is reduced to the swoon theory alone.
3. **V**, if "embodied" is read so narrowly that Pauline "spiritual body" readings are folded into R, or so broadly that V disappears.
4. **F**, as above.
5. **Gnostic or "spiritual resurrection" readings**, which are interpretive claims about what "resurrection" meant. They should live inside V or L, not be excluded.

## F. Scope and design attacks

1. **Q1 is compound.** It bundles death, post-mortem embodiment, encounter reality and causal role in originating the proclamation. It needs decomposition into necessary propositions. Otherwise partial truth, such as real encounters but not embodied, can be mis-scored.
2. **Heterogeneous appearances.** If Paul's experience, the Twelve, and group episodes differ in kind, Q1 could be "true" for a subset. A threshold (how many or which episodes must be embodied-encounter) must be preregistered.
3. **"Earliest proclamation" and "earliest experience reports" are ambiguous.** They could mean the Jerusalem group, Galilee, or Paul's network. Define them.
4. **Vocabulary.** "Appearance" presumes veridicality. "Hallucination" presumes pathology. "Resurrection" in some uses presumes bodily revival. Use neutral terms such as "experience report" and "claimed encounter".
5. **False dichotomy risk.** "Resurrection vs hallucination" ignores V, L, S and F.
6. **Overlap.**
   - V and R overlap on "real encounter".
   - H and L overlap on "experience in origin".
   - The distinguishing necessary propositions must be explicit.
7. **Sincerity, the unexplained and possibility.**
   - Sincerity is not truth.
   - An unexplained residue is not supernatural evidence.
   - Metaphysical possibility is not historical probability.
8. **Source labels.** Canonical vs non-canonical is not source quality. Consider Gospel of Peter, Ignatius, the Josephan testimonium and second-century material by claim-specific proximity, dependence and reliability.
9. **Q2 gating.** Q2 is conditional on Q1 warrant. This is asymmetric unless Q2 gets its own preregistered candidate and background set. Consider two separately preregistered comparisons, one for Q1 and one for Q2.
10. **Interaction with excluded material.** Q2 attribution may implicitly lean on Jesus's life or claims, which is outside scope. Fix this boundary in advance.
