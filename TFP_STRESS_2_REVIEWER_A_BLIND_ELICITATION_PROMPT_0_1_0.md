## 3. Truth question

### Q1 — Event proposition

> Does the best publicly adjudicable historical evidence warrant the proposition that Jesus of Nazareth genuinely died by crucifixion and subsequently existed again in an embodied living state, such that a real postmortem embodied encounter process materially caused the earliest Christian resurrection proclamation and experience reports?

"Embodied living state" is stronger than:
- dream;
- bereavement vision;
- purely internal religious experience;
- literary symbol;
- memory construction;
- apparition with no embodied referent;
- survival from crucifixion without death.

It does not by itself establish the later full doctrinal content of Christianity.

### Q2 — Causal/agent attribution

Only if Q1 receives sufficient warrant to make causal attribution meaningful:

> What causal class, if any, is warranted for the event, and does the evidence warrant the further proposition that God raised Jesus from the dead?

Q2 must preserve ordinary causes, unknown cause, unspecified anomaly/supernatural cause, and specific divine action as distinct possibilities where applicable.

A positive Q1 does not mechanically imply a positive Q2.
A positive Q2 does not mechanically establish Christianity as a whole.

## 4. Generic scope

- Primary event window: the crucifixion period in the early first century CE and the emergence of the earliest resurrection proclamation/appearance traditions.
- Source-evaluation window: first-century material plus early second-century material through approximately 150 CE where it bears on transmission, dependence, reception, or development.
- Primary geography: Roman Judea/Galilee, Jerusalem, and early eastern Mediterranean Christian networks.
- Relevant languages may include Greek, Aramaic/Hebrew substrate claims, and Latin.
- Later sources may illuminate transmission/reception but may not be silently projected backward.
- Canonical status, skeptical orientation, confessional status, tradition, popularity, or institutional prestige may not function as evidence by authority.

Explicitly outside the direct scope:
- Christianity as a whole;
- Trinity;
- complete incarnation doctrine;
- atonement theories;
- global biblical inspiration/inerrancy;
- denominational authority;
- unrelated prophecy fulfillment;
- all miracles attributed to Jesus;
- all later Christian miracle claims;
- total harmonization of every narrative detail.
