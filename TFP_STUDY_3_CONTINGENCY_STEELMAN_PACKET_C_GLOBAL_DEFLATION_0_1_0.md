# TFP-STUDY-3-CONTINGENCY — B3 Steelman Packet: C-GLOBAL-DEFLATION 0.1.0

## Identity

- Candidate: `C-GLOBAL-DEFLATION`
- Frozen label: **Global-explanation deflationism**
- Status: **FROZEN_B3_PACKET__PENDING_EQUAL_STRENGTH_REVIEW**

## Candidate-native / primary formulation

Genuine contingency may obtain locally or throughout a domain, yet no truth-bearing rule automatically converts the availability or appropriateness of local explanations into a further demand for one explanation of contingent reality “as a whole.” Once the relevant parts, relations, or local facts are adequately explained, a further totality-level why-question may add no legitimate explanatory burden.

## Literature status

This is a direct family of objections to cosmological arguments, especially Humean and Russellian resistance to a distinct explanation of the whole. It overlaps with brute-fact views but is not identical: its core claim concerns the legitimacy of the **global explanatory demand**, not simply the existence of an unexplained stopping fact.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **David Hume**, *Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion*, Part IX (1779): once the individual members of a series are explained, Hume challenges the demand for an additional cause of the whole.
2. **Bertrand Russell**, 1948 BBC debate with F. C. Copleston: Russell resists treating “the universe” as something that automatically requires a further explanatory cause.
3. *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, “Cosmological Argument,” section discussing the Hume-Edwards style objection that explaining constituents may be sufficient.

## Core truth propositions

1. Relevant local or domain contingency can be genuine.
2. Local explanatory demands do not automatically aggregate into a distinct totality-level explanatory demand.
3. The absence of a global ground is not by itself an explanatory defect.
4. The family does not depend on denying metaphysical contingency itself.

## Authority commitments

None. The position depends on a theory of explanatory scope, composition, and question legitimacy.

## Internal success conditions

- The local/global distinction must be principled rather than ad hoc.
- The view must show why a totality-level question is redundant, malformed, or unsupported.
- It must allow genuine local explanation rather than global skepticism about explanation.
- It must remain distinct from C-BRUTE: the key claim is not simply “the whole is brute.”
- It must remain distinct from C-MODAL-DISSOLUTION: ordinary metaphysical contingency may remain fully truth-apt.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

The view expects explanatory success to be exhausted by the right local/domain explanations, without an additional empirically detectable “global explainer.” Its main support is philosophical analysis of part/whole explanation, composition, and the conditions under which a why-question is legitimate.

No G1 result is presupposed.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Big contingent fact objection:** even if every member has an explanation, the fact that this totality of contingent facts obtains may itself be contingent.
2. **Hume-Edwards objection:** critics argue that member-wise explanation does not necessarily explain why there are any such members or why the entire contingent conjunction obtains.
3. **Scope asymmetry objection:** refusing the global question while accepting structurally similar local questions may require independent justification.
4. **Brute-collapse objection:** if there genuinely is a contingent totality fact and it lacks explanation, the view may reduce to C-BRUTE.
5. **Ontological-whole objection:** the claim that the whole is merely a conceptual collection may be false for some candidate ontologies.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- The family does not deny contingency.
- It does not say local explanations are impossible.
- It does not necessarily say the universe is a brute fact.
- It does not depend on naturalism.
- It does not assert that “whole” is meaningless in every context.
