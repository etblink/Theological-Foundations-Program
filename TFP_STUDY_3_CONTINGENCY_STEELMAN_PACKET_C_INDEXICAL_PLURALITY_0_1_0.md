# TFP-STUDY-3-CONTINGENCY — B3 Steelman Packet: C-INDEXICAL-PLURALITY 0.1.0

## Identity

- Candidate: `C-INDEXICAL-PLURALITY`
- Frozen label: **Indexical contingency in a necessary plurality**
- Status: **FROZEN_B3_PACKET__PENDING_EQUAL_STRENGTH_REVIEW**

## Candidate-native / primary formulation

This locus or world may genuinely be contingent from its own indexical standpoint while a broader plurality of loci or possibilities supplies the stopping structure. No external selector is required to choose this member; “this” or “actual” picks out our location within the plurality rather than identifying a metaphysically privileged selected world.

## Literature status

This is a **synthetic TFP family** whose strongest anchor is Lewisian modal realism: a concrete plurality of worlds on an ontological par and an indexical account of actuality. The frozen TFP thesis goes beyond Lewis by treating the plurality itself as the relevant stopping structure. The packet therefore does not attribute that additional ultimacy claim to Lewis without qualification.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **David Lewis**, *On the Plurality of Worlds* (Blackwell, 1986): concrete possible worlds are ontologically on a par and actuality is indexical.
2. *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*, “The Possibilism–Actualism Debate,” supplement on Lewisian possibilism, for the canonical statement of Lewis's plurality and indexical actuality.
3. Contemporary discussion of “advanced modalizing” is relevant because it asks whether the plurality of worlds itself could have been otherwise; that problem is directly material to TFP’s added stopping-structure claim.

## Core truth propositions

1. This locus/world can be contingent at the indexical level.
2. A broader plurality of loci or possibilities exists as the relevant stopping structure.
3. No external selector is needed to make this locus uniquely actual.
4. “Actual” or its analogue is indexical rather than a metaphysically privileged selection relation.
5. The plurality is not itself a further contingent remainder requiring an external ground.

## Authority commitments

None. The family is metaphysical and modal.

## Internal success conditions

- A coherent plurality ontology must be specified.
- Indexical actuality must remove the need for an external selector rather than merely hiding one.
- The plurality's own modal status must be addressed.
- The view must preserve genuine locus-level contingency.
- It must not collapse into C-NECESSITARIAN by making this locus itself necessary.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

The strongest support is theoretical: a successful account of modality and actuality in which plenitude explains why no selector is needed. Candidate-native empirical predictions are not fixed merely by the plurality thesis.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Ontological-cost objection:** a vast concrete plurality can be seen as extravagant.
2. **Advanced-modalizing objection:** even if worlds explain ordinary modality, what fixes the plurality itself and its extent?
3. **Stopping-structure objection:** TFP’s claim that the plurality needs no external ground requires independent defense beyond Lewis's indexical theory.
4. **Measure/typicality objection:** plenitude may generate new questions about why observers occupy this locus or what probabilistic measure applies.
5. **Explanatory-relevance objection:** removing a selector may not explain why the plurality exists.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- The family does not say this world is necessary.
- It does not require a personal multiverse selector.
- It is not simply the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics.
- It does not claim Lewis himself explicitly solved the ultimacy question in TFP's form.
- “Indexical” does not mean subjective or unreal.

## Packet contamination note

Lewis supplies the strongest native machinery; the necessary/plural stopping-role is explicitly marked as TFP's broader synthesis rather than silently imported into Lewis's view.
