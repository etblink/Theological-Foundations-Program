# TFP-STUDY-3-CONTINGENCY — B3 Steelman Packet: C-AXIARCHIC 0.1.0

## Identity

- Candidate: `C-AXIARCHIC`
- Frozen label: **Non-agentive axiological or optimality requiredness**
- Status: **FROZEN_B3_PACKET__PENDING_EQUAL_STRENGTH_REVIEW**

## Candidate-native / primary formulation

A necessary value, goodness, optimality, or related evaluative principle non-agentively requires or explains why relevant contingent reality obtains. Evaluative requiredness itself performs explanatory work. No personal chooser or intentional selector is built into the family.

The family therefore differs both from C-NEC-AGENTIVE and from a generic impersonal principle whose explanatory work is non-evaluative.

## Literature status

Axiarchism is a direct philosophical position with a recognizable literature. TFP adopts its impersonal explanatory core while refusing to equate evaluative requiredness with the perfect goodness of a personal agent.

## Strongest serious proponent / native sources

1. **John Leslie**, *Value and Existence* (Rowman & Littlefield, 1979), a foundational modern statement of the idea that ethical/value requiredness can have creative explanatory force.
2. **Tim Mulgan**, “Beyond Theism and Atheism: Axiarchism and Ananthropocentric Purposivism,” *Philosophy Compass* 12.6 (2017), discussing axiarchists including Leslie, Derek Parfit, and Nicholas Rescher and the proposed direct connection between goodness and existence.
3. **W. J. Mander**, “The Axiarchic Argument,” in *Idealist Ethics* (Oxford University Press, 2016), pp. 87–99, examining Leslie and Rescher and the idea that goodness can explain the existence of the universe.
4. **Tim Mawson**, “Why Is There Anything at All?” (2008/2009), treating axiarchism as a serious competitor to theistic and other answers to the existence question.

## Core truth propositions

1. A necessary evaluative or optimality truth/principle exists or obtains.
2. Value/requiredness has genuine explanatory force with respect to existence or actuality.
3. The explanatory work is non-agentive.
4. Evaluative requiredness is not merely a reason an independently existing agent happens to act.
5. Personhood, agency, perfect goodness of a person, and religious identity do not follow from the family itself.

## Authority commitments

None. The family requires objective evaluative structure, but not a religious revelation or divine command.

## Internal success conditions

- Objective value/requiredness must be coherent and sufficiently determinate.
- A bridge from value to actuality/existence must be supplied rather than asserted.
- The explanatory relation must be genuinely non-agentive.
- The account must address whether a best or uniquely required world exists.
- If value ceases to be explanatorily essential, the position collapses into C-NEC-IMPERSONAL-PRINCIPLE.

## Expected evidence / argumentative profile

The family expects the existence or character of reality to be intelligible partly in evaluative terms: actuality should bear the relation to objective value that the theory says has creative or grounding force. But no particular fine-tuning, moral, or theological datum is frozen here as confirmatory evidence.

## Strongest recognized objections

1. **Value-to-being bridge objection:** why should goodness, value, or “ought” possess any power to explain what exists?
2. **Best-world objection:** there may be no uniquely best possible world, or there may be indefinitely improving alternatives.
3. **Problem-of-evil / disvalue objection:** extensive disvalue may sit poorly with strong forms of axiarchic optimization.
4. **Normative-realist burden:** the view requires objective evaluative facts strong enough to do metaphysical work.
5. **Hidden-agent objection:** language of “selection,” “preference,” or “creative goodness” can covertly reintroduce agency unless carefully cashed out non-agentively.
6. **Principle-collapse objection:** if value is only one label for a generic necessary law, the family may reduce to C-NEC-IMPERSONAL-PRINCIPLE.

## Common caricatures explicitly rejected

- Axiarchism is not automatically theism.
- It does not posit a morally perfect person.
- It is not an independent moral argument for God.
- “Goodness explains existence” does not mean a conscious value-force makes choices.
- The family need not claim this world maximizes human welfare.
- It does not establish any religion's account of creation.

## Packet contamination note

The packet follows the frozen boundary: axiarchic requiredness is impersonal and non-agentive. Literature using theological language is not allowed to import personhood or divine perfection into the candidate.
