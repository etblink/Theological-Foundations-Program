# Research Seed 0.1.0

**Project:** Theological Foundations Program  
**Status:** pre-inception  
**Epistemic posture:** comparative, source-sensitive, argument-sensitive, non-convergence-presuming

## 1. Motivation

The project is intended to provide theology with a disciplined research program analogous in seriousness—but not necessarily in method—to comparative work previously undertaken in physics.

The motivating question is not:

> How can an already preferred theology be defended?

It is closer to:

> What theological claims and frameworks survive careful comparison against the kinds of evidence and argument actually relevant to them?

## 2. Why a separate methodology is required

Theology is not simply physics with different nouns.

Its source base can include:

- ancient texts;
- manuscript traditions;
- historical testimony;
- interpretive traditions;
- philosophical argument;
- archaeology;
- linguistic evidence;
- institutional history;
- lived religious practice;
- claims of revelation;
- metaphysical reasoning.

Different claims therefore require different standards.

A claim about manuscript transmission should not be judged by the same method as a claim about the existence of God.

A claim about what Paul meant should not be treated as identical to a claim about whether Paul's theology is true.

A doctrinal development can be historically traceable without thereby being theologically correct.

## 3. Initial object of study

The program may begin with Christian theology because of the user's existing interest and because it supplies a large, historically rich, internally diverse body of claims.

That does not authorize:

- assuming Christianity is true;
- assuming Christianity is false;
- assuming one denomination is normative;
- assuming later doctrinal consensus perfectly preserves earliest belief;
- or treating minority positions as correct merely because they are minority positions.

## 4. Candidate research layers

### Textual layer

Questions of manuscripts, variants, translation, genre, authorship, composition, and textual meaning.

### Historical layer

Questions of people, events, institutions, practices, chronology, transmission, and development.

### Interpretive layer

Competing readings of texts and traditions.

### Philosophical layer

Arguments concerning God, metaphysics, morality, mind, causation, necessity, contingency, evil, freedom, and related topics.

### Doctrinal layer

How theological propositions cohere within systems and how those systems relate to source material.

### Comparative layer

How rival traditions or frameworks answer the same question and what assumptions drive their disagreement.

### Existential / practical layer

How theological claims shape worship, ethics, community, vocation, suffering, hope, and lived practice.

This layer should not be confused with evidential proof.

## 5. Candidate principles

### Equal-standard principle

Competing positions should be held to comparable evidential and argumentative standards where the claims are genuinely comparable.

### Claim-type principle

Standards must match the type of claim being evaluated.

### Source-proximity principle

Later interpretations should not silently replace earlier sources when the research question concerns earlier sources.

### Negative-evidence principle

Failure of an argument, prophecy interpretation, historical claim, or doctrinal derivation should be preserved rather than hidden.

### Steelman principle

A view should be tested in a form its informed proponents could recognize.

### Scope principle

A result about one doctrine, passage, tradition, or argument should not automatically generalize to an entire religion.

### Belief/research distinction

Personal belief may motivate inquiry but should not determine research disposition.

## 6. Important distinctions

The program should repeatedly distinguish:

- historical plausibility from theological truth;
- textual meaning from doctrinal use;
- doctrinal coherence from external truth;
- philosophical possibility from actuality;
- explanatory power from proof;
- tradition from original source;
- consensus from correctness;
- faith from evidential warrant;
- absence of evidence from evidence of absence.

## 7. Initial comparison possibilities

Potential future comparative studies include:

- Trinitarian models;
- Christology;
- atonement;
- resurrection claims;
- divine hiddenness;
- problem of evil;
- free will and providence;
- scriptural authority;
- canon;
- sacramental theology;
- justification and sanctification;
- ecclesiology;
- eschatology;
- moral realism;
- natural theology;
- revelation and miracle claims;
- denominational disagreements;
- Jewish-Christian interpretive continuity and discontinuity.

This list is illustrative, not a roadmap.

## 8. Relationship to Invariant Structure Research

Theological systems may contain structures such as:

- hierarchy;
- identity and distinction;
- relation;
- unity and plurality;
- causation;
- representation;
- covenant;
- law and exception;
- continuity across transmission;
- canonicalization;
- interpretive transformation;
- local tradition versus global claim.

These are candidates for structural comparison only.

No similarity to another project establishes theological truth.

## 9. Anti-apologetic-drift safeguard

The project may produce arguments that support Christian claims.

It may also produce results that weaken or complicate them.

A research result should not be rejected merely because it is inconvenient for apologetics.

Likewise, skeptical arguments should not receive a lower evidential burden merely because they oppose a religious claim.

## 10. Anti-reduction safeguard

Theology should not be reduced to sociology, psychology, physics, literary criticism, or philosophy merely because those disciplines illuminate part of it.

Likewise, theological language should not be used as a substitute for empirical explanation where empirical explanation is required.

## 11. Pre-inception boundary

At version 0.1.0, this repository preserves intent rather than implementing a mature method.

Not yet fixed:

- canonical source corpus;
- denomination or tradition priority;
- scoring system;
- framework taxonomy;
- adjudication procedure;
- literature-review protocol;
- treatment of confessional commitments;
- order of research questions;
- publication format.

Those should be designed explicitly before major comparative claims are produced.

## 12. Seed principle

> Seek the strongest account of what is true, while keeping textual, historical, philosophical, doctrinal, empirical, and faith claims distinct enough that one cannot silently substitute for another.
